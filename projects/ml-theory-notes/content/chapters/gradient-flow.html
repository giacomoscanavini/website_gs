A derivative travels through a product

For a multilayer network, an early sensitivity contains products of weight matrices and activation derivatives. The guide’s scalar illustration uses ten activation derivatives of 0.20.2 :

0.210=1.024×10−7.0.2^{10}=1.024\times10^{-7}.

Even an order-one signal can become tiny after enough contractions. This illustration isolates the activation contribution; real network behavior also depends on weight matrices, their orientations, and the activations encountered on the actual input.

The opposite problem occurs when the effective transformations amplify sensitivity repeatedly. An exploding gradient can cause an enormous update, numerical instability, or an unusable training trajectory. Depth and recurrence both create long derivative paths, which is why the same issue appears in feed-forward networks and in backpropagation through time.

Step by step Expand a gradient path and identify what can shrink

Setting. a[ℓ]=ϕ(Wℓa[ℓ−1]+bℓ)a^{[\ell]}=\phi(W_\ell a^{[\ell-1]}+b_\ell) . Let DℓD_\ell be the diagonal matrix of activation derivatives at that observation. We use column gradients and the Euclidean operator norm.

1 · Write one forward Jacobian

The local derivative is Jℓ=∂a[ℓ]/∂a[ℓ−1]=DℓWℓJ_\ell=\partial a^{[\ell]}/\partial a^{[\ell-1]}=D_\ell W_\ell . The backward sensitivity is consequently

∇a[ℓ−1]L=Jℓ⊤∇a[ℓ]L=Wℓ⊤Dℓ∇a[ℓ]L.\nabla_{a^{[\ell-1]}}L=J_\ell^\top\nabla_{a^{[\ell]}}L=W_\ell^\top D_\ell\nabla_{a^{[\ell]}}L.

2 · Substitute repeatedly with the order intact

For three layers,

∇xL=J1⊤J2⊤J3⊤∇a[3]L.\nabla_xL=J_1^\top J_2^\top J_3^\top\nabla_{a^{[3]}}L.

The factors generally do not commute. Reversing their order changes the derivative.

For any compatible matrix and vector, ∥Av∥≤∥A∥∥v∥\|Av\|\le\|A\|\|v\| . Thus

∥∇xL∥≤∏ℓ=1L∥Jℓ∥∥∇a[L]L∥≤∏ℓ=1L∥Dℓ∥∥Wℓ∥∥∇a[L]L∥.\|\nabla_xL\|\le\prod_{\ell=1}^L\|J_\ell\|\,\|\nabla_{a^{[L]}}L\| \le\prod_{\ell=1}^L\|D_\ell\|\|W_\ell\|\,\|\nabla_{a^{[L]}}L\|.

If every ∥Jℓ∥≤r<1\|J_\ell\|\le r<1 , this upper bound is rLr^L times the final sensitivity.

4 · Interpret a large upper bound carefully

A product of norms above one does not prove the actual gradient explodes: directions may rotate, or a matrix may contract the particular vector it receives. Repeated amplification along the realized path can cause explosion; a loose bound merely permits it.

5 · Substitute the scalar illustration

With ten scalar factors all equal to 0.20.2 , the product is

0.210=1510=19765625=1.024×10−7.0.2^{10}=\frac1{5^{10}}=\frac1{9765625}=1.024\times10^{-7}.

This calculation isolates activation factors. Active ReLU replaces its factor by one; it does not remove the weight matrices or inactive branches from the full path.

Active ReLU is helpful, not sufficient

On a positive pre-activation, ReLU has derivative one. It removes activation saturation from that branch. But a sequence of contracting weight transformations can still shrink gradients, and negative ReLU branches still produce zero sensitivity. The claim “ReLU has derivative one” is incomplete without its active-region qualification.

Leaky ReLU gives a small negative-branch slope. Gated recurrent models introduce an additive memory path. Residual architectures preserve a comparatively direct identity path. These address different aspects of information and gradient flow; none guarantees useful long-term learning in all settings.

Symmetry breaking

If all hidden units in a layer start with the same incoming weights and biases and are treated identically by the rest of the model, they can produce the same activations and receive the same updates. Instead of learning several distinct features, the units behave like copies. Random initialization breaks this symmetry.

Randomness alone is not enough. Very large initial weights can push sigmoid/tanh units into saturation or amplify signals. Very small weights can contract activations and gradients. Initialization is therefore both a diversity problem and a scale problem.

Fan-in, variance, and activation choice

The source gives He initialization for ReLU networks:

Wij∼𝒩(0,2nin).W_{ij}\sim\mathcal N\left(0,\frac{2}{n_{\mathrm{in}}}\right).

Under independent zero-mean initial weights, a weighted sum has a second moment proportional to fan-in times the weight variance and input second moment. For a symmetric pre-activation distribution, ReLU halves the second moment, not generally the centered variance: its output usually has a nonzero mean. Preserving that second moment gives the He factor 2/nin2/n_{\mathrm{in}} . These are initialization assumptions, not identities that every trained layer must satisfy.

For sigmoid or tanh, the source describes Xavier/Glorot initialization with a scale of order 1/nin1/n_{\mathrm{in}} or a related fan-in/fan-out expression. The linked initialization reference gives exact PyTorch choices, gains, and weight-orientation assumptions. These assumptions matter more than memorizing an initializer’s name.

Assume independent zero-mean weights of variance vwv_w , independent of input activations, zero biases, and comparable activation second moments q=𝔼[aj2]q=\mathbb E[a_j^2] . These are initialization approximations, not permanent training properties.

1 · Expand the second moment of a weighted sum

For zi=∑j=1nWijajz_i=\sum_{j=1}^nW_{ij}a_j ,

𝔼[zi2]=∑j𝔼[Wij2aj2]+∑j≠k𝔼[WijWikajak].\mathbb E[z_i^2]=\sum_j\mathbb E[W_{ij}^2a_j^2]+\sum_{j\ne k}\mathbb E[W_{ij}W_{ik}a_ja_k].

2 · Evaluate the effect of ReLU

If the distribution of zz is symmetric around zero,

𝔼[ReLU(z)2]=∫0∞z2p(z)dz=12𝔼[z2].\mathbb E[\operatorname{ReLU}(z)^2]=\int_0^\infty z^2p(z)\,dz=\frac12\mathbb E[z^2].

The factor halves the second moment , not generally the centered variance. ReLU usually has a nonzero mean.

3 · Preserve the activation second moment across a layer

The next moment is qnext=nvwq/2q_{\mathrm{next}}=nv_wq/2 . Setting qnext=qq_{\mathrm{next}}=q for nonzero qq gives

vw=2n.v_w=\frac2n.

This is the He fan-in scale for ReLU. For Leaky ReLU with negative slope α\alpha , the positive and negative halves contribute (1+α2)/2(1+\alpha^2)/2 , giving vw=2/[n(1+α2)]v_w=2/[n(1+\alpha^2)] .

4 · Compare forward and backward requirements for a linearized activation

For a near-linear layer, forward second-moment preservation suggests nvw=1nv_w=1 . Backpropagation sums over mm outputs, so under an analogous independence approximation the backward requirement is mvw=1mv_w=1 . Both can hold exactly with one variance only when m=nm=n .

5 · Form the Xavier compromise

Using average fan size (n+m)/2(n+m)/2 gives

vw=2n+m.v_w=\frac2{n+m}.

A symmetric uniform distribution on [−a,a][-a,a] has variance a2/3a^2/3 : integrating w2/(2a)w^2/(2a) from −a-a to aa gives a2/3a^2/3 . Thus the unit-gain Xavier uniform bound is a=6/(n+m)a=\sqrt{6/(n+m)} . Activation-specific gains change this scale; framework defaults and initialization assumptions should be checked separately.

Clipping changes a large gradient, not the objective

Global-norm clipping uses

g̃={g∥g∥≤c,cg/∥g∥∥g∥>c,c>0.\tilde g=\begin{cases}g&\lVert g\rVert\le c,\\ cg/\lVert g\rVert&\lVert g\rVert>c,\end{cases}\qquad c>0.

When the norm is below cc , it leaves the gradient alone. Otherwise it rescales all coordinates by a common factor and preserves the direction. The optimizer then consumes this modified gradient. In adaptive optimizers, the resulting parameter update also depends on stored moments, so a gradient norm threshold is not an identical bound on every update norm.

Clipping limits explosions. It does not recover information already lost through a vanishing derivative, and it does not repair a wrong loss, a leakage problem, or missing connectivity in the computational graph.

A useful diagnostic order

The guides suggest inspecting learning rate, initialization, gradient magnitudes, model shape, and objective consistency when a more expressive network unexpectedly fails. Ask whether early layers receive a useful derivative, whether units are mostly inactive or saturated, and whether changes in parameter scale are sensible. Only then interpret depth as the source of the problem.

The architecture chapters return to this theme: LSTM memory provides a gated additive path, while Transformers stabilize repeated blocks with residual connections and normalization.

Check your understanding Why is initializing all hidden weights to zero problematic?

Hidden units can begin with identical outputs and receive identical updates, so they continue learning the same representation instead of different features. Random initialization breaks that symmetry. Its scale must also be chosen to avoid shrinking or exploding activations and gradients.
