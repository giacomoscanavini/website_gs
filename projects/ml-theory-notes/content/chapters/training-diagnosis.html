First identify which part of the learning process is failing

The guides distinguish at least three possibilities: the model cannot represent what matters, the optimization procedure is not reaching a useful parameter region, or the fitted function fails to generalize. Similar-looking final scores can arise for different reasons.

For example, high errors on both training and validation can reflect underfitting, difficult optimization, inadequate inputs, or too much regularization. A tiny training error with weak validation is instead consistent with overfitting. More dropout is not a universal answer, and a more powerful optimizer is not a universal answer either.

These are hypotheses, not diagnoses guaranteed by a curve. Inspect the underlying computation before treating the pattern as proof.

Monitor the update, not only the loss

The optimization PDF suggests monitoring

Jt,∥gt∥,∥Δθt∥,∥Δθt∥∥θt∥+ϵ.J_t,\qquad \lVert g_t\rVert,\qquad\lVert\Delta\theta_t\rVert,\qquad \frac{\lVert\Delta\theta_t\rVert}{\lVert\theta_t\rVert+\epsilon}.

The last ratio asks whether an update is small or large relative to the existing parameter scale. A tiny gradient is not automatically harmless; it might be a symptom of saturation. A large gradient is not automatically a useful learning signal; it might be an instability. Interpret each quantity with the activation and objective scales.

The official TensorBoard tutorial already demonstrates graph, data, and training visualizations. Use it to inspect a real run rather than reconstruct another dashboard inside these notes.

Step by step Derive global-norm clipping as a projection

Setting. A finite gradient vector g∈ℝdg\in\mathbb R^d and radius c>0c>0 . Seek the vector closest to gg inside the Euclidean ball:

minu12∥u−g∥2subject to ∥u∥≤c.\min_u\frac12\|u-g\|^2\quad\text{subject to }\|u\|\le c.

1 · Check the feasible unconstrained optimum

The derivative of 12∥u−g∥2\tfrac12\|u-g\|^2 is u−gu-g . Its unconstrained minimum is u=gu=g . If ∥g∥≤c\|g\|\le c , this is feasible and already solves the constrained problem. In particular g=0g=0 stays zero; no division is required.

2 · Handle a gradient outside the ball

If ∥g∥>c\|g\|>c , the optimum lies on its boundary. Use the Lagrangian

ℒ(u,λ)=12∥u−g∥2+λ2(∥u∥2−c2),λ≥0.\mathcal L(u,\lambda)=\tfrac12\|u-g\|^2+\tfrac\lambda2(\|u\|^2-c^2),\qquad\lambda\ge0.

Stationarity gives u−g+λu=0u-g+\lambda u=0 , hence u=g/(1+λ)u=g/(1+\lambda) .

3 · Enforce the boundary radius

∥u∥=∥g∥1+λ=c⇒1+λ=∥g∥c.\|u\|=\frac{\|g\|}{1+\lambda}=c\quad\Rightarrow\quad 1+\lambda=\frac{\|g\|}{c}.

Substituting yields u=cg/∥g∥u=cg/\|g\| .

4 · Combine the two cases

g̃={g∥g∥≤c,cg/∥g∥∥g∥>c.\tilde g=\begin{cases}g&\|g\|\le c,\\ cg/\|g\|&\|g\|>c.\end{cases}

For nonzero gg , this is gmin(1,c/∥g∥)g\min(1,c/\|g\|) . Norm clipping preserves direction on the clipped branch; clipping each coordinate independently does not generally do so.

5 · Distinguish gradient and update bounds

For basic SGD, Δθ=−ηg̃\Delta\theta=-\eta\tilde g implies ∥Δθ∥≤ηc\|\Delta\theta\|\le\eta c . With momentum or adaptive state, the update also depends on previous history. Bounding the fresh gradient alone does not establish the same bound on the final parameter displacement.

Architecture mistakes can masquerade as optimization problems

The basics guide lists missing hidden activations, inappropriate loss/output combinations, bad initialization, tensor-shape mistakes, and inadvertently detached tensors. A deeper network that fails where a simpler one succeeds should first be checked for these issues.

Check a small forward calculation, verify that each output has the intended meaning, and confirm that gradients connect the loss to the relevant parameters. The running forward/backward example provides a compact numerical reference for what a correct chain should look like.

Step by step Derive a directional finite-difference gradient check

Setting. Hold the data and all randomness fixed. Let vv be a unit direction and assume JJ is sufficiently smooth along θ+tv\theta+tv . A directional check compares g⊤vg^\top v with a scalar finite difference.

1 · Write both Taylor expansions

J(θ+hv)=J(θ)+hg⊤v+h22v⊤Hv+O(h3),J(\theta+hv)=J(\theta)+h g^\top v+\frac{h^2}{2}v^\top Hv+O(h^3),
J(θ−hv)=J(θ)−hg⊤v+h22v⊤Hv+O(h3).J(\theta-hv)=J(\theta)-h g^\top v+\frac{h^2}{2}v^\top Hv+O(h^3).

2 · Subtract to cancel the even-order terms

The constant and quadratic terms cancel, leaving

J(θ+hv)−J(θ−hv)=2hg⊤v+O(h3).J(\theta+hv)-J(\theta-hv)=2h g^\top v+O(h^3).

Divide by 2h2h :

J(θ+hv)−J(θ−hv)2h=g⊤v+O(h2).\frac{J(\theta+hv)-J(\theta-hv)}{2h}=g^\top v+O(h^2).

The centered check has second-order truncation error.

3 · Explain why a smaller step is not always better

Finite-precision evaluations subtract nearly equal numbers. A rough roundoff contribution scales like ϵmachine|J|/h\epsilon_{\mathrm{machine}}|J|/h , while truncation scales like h2h^2 . Making hh arbitrarily small trades one error for the other; use a range of sensible steps and double precision for a numerical check.

4 · Respect nonsmooth and stochastic computations

At a ReLU kink, the classical derivative may not exist, and centered differences need not match a chosen subgradient. Different stochastic masks evaluate different functions, invalidating the subtraction. Avoid kink crossings and freeze randomness before interpreting a discrepancy as a backpropagation error.

5 · Use checks as evidence, not a proof

A passing directional check supports correctness along that direction at that parameter point. Multiple independent directions, analytically tractable cases, and shape tests provide stronger coverage. They do not prove every possible input and branch is correct.

Regularization effects can accumulate

Strong weight decay, high dropout, aggressive augmentation, and early stopping all restrict the learned solution in different ways. Their combined effect can produce underfitting. The regularization PDF explicitly warns against adding more constraints when both training and validation remain weak.

Augmentation also needs a credible target-preservation assumption. A model trained on corrupted labels may look difficult to optimize when the real problem is that its effective training task is inconsistent.

Compare complete procedures fairly

Optimizer names alone do not define experiments. Base rate, schedule, batch size, momentum, decay, and stopping criteria all affect outcomes. BFGS and CG additionally depend heavily on line search and curvature safeguards. Tune within a validation procedure and compare computation as well as validation performance.

The validation set used to choose those settings is part of model selection. Repeatedly choosing settings from the test score changes the role of the test data. For temporal problems, the guides emphasize time ordering and resampling schemes that preserve the intended dependence.

A practical order is to establish a correct objective and derivative path, obtain reliable training behavior, and then address the gap to unseen data. Changing many components simultaneously makes that reasoning harder, because a better result no longer reveals which change addressed the failure.

Check your understanding Training loss is low but validation loss is poor. Is a faster optimizer the first remedy?

Not necessarily. That pattern points toward a generalization problem rather than inability to fit the training sample. Check evaluation consistency and leakage, then consider capacity, regularization, augmentation, and early stopping. Faster training alone can fit the unstable structure sooner.
