Three meanings of “sparse”

A parameter vector is sparse when many learned coefficients are zero. A representation is sparse when an example uses only a few latent features. A dropout mask creates temporary zeros in a computation. Similar-looking zeros do not make the mechanisms equivalent.

For sparse coding, the guide writes x≈Dax\approx Da , where DD is a dictionary and aa is the code. The code solves

â∈argmina[12∥x−Da∥2+λ∥a∥1].\hat a\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_a\left[\frac12\lVert x-Da\rVert^2+\lambda\lVert a\rVert_1\right].

The dictionary can have more columns than input dimensions; each example still uses only a small subset. This is representation sparsity , not necessarily a sparse dictionary or a sparse network parameter matrix.

Rarely active hidden units

A sparse autoencoder can penalize average hidden activity. If ρ̂j\hat\rho_j is unit jj ’s observed mean activity and ρ\rho is the desired small activity, one penalty is

∑jKL(ρ‖ρ̂j),\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(\rho\Vert\hat\rho_j),

where the Bernoulli divergence is

KL(ρ‖ρ̂)=ρlogρρ̂+(1−ρ)log1−ρ1−ρ̂.\operatorname{KL}(\rho\Vert\hat\rho)=\rho\log\frac\rho{\hat\rho}+(1-\rho)\log\frac{1-\rho}{1-\hat\rho}.

This expression assumes the activity quantities have the probability-like interpretation required by the Bernoulli divergence. It should not be applied mechanically to unrestricted hidden values. The aim is for units to be useful selectively across examples.

Step by step Differentiate the sparse-activation KL penalty all the way to one activation

Setting. A hidden unit has activations a1,…,aB∈(0,1)a_1,\ldots,a_B\in(0,1) and empirical mean ρ̂=B−1∑iai\hat\rho=B^{-1}\sum_i a_i . A target activity ρ∈(0,1)\rho\in(0,1) is fixed. Bernoulli KL requires both probabilities to lie strictly between zero and one for this finite formula.

1 · Write the two possible Bernoulli outcomes

For outcome one the probabilities are ρ\rho and ρ̂\hat\rho ; for outcome zero they are 1−ρ1-\rho and 1−ρ̂1-\hat\rho . By the definition of discrete KL,

K(ρ̂)=ρlogρρ̂+(1−ρ)log1−ρ1−ρ̂.K(\hat\rho)=\rho\log\frac\rho{\hat\rho}+(1-\rho)\log\frac{1-\rho}{1-\hat\rho}.

2 · Separate constant and variable logarithms

K=ρlogρ−ρlogρ̂+(1−ρ)log(1−ρ)−(1−ρ)log(1−ρ̂).K=\rho\log\rho-\rho\log\hat\rho+(1-\rho)\log(1-\rho)-(1-\rho)\log(1-\hat\rho).

Differentiate with respect to ρ̂\hat\rho :

K′(ρ̂)=−ρρ̂+1−ρ1−ρ̂=ρ̂−ρρ̂(1−ρ̂).K'(\hat\rho)=-\frac\rho{\hat\rho}+\frac{1-\rho}{1-\hat\rho} =\frac{\hat\rho-\rho}{\hat\rho(1-\hat\rho)}.

The denominator is positive, so the derivative points toward the target mean.

3 · Verify the minimum

The derivative is zero at ρ̂=ρ\hat\rho=\rho , and

K″(ρ̂)=ρρ̂2+1−ρ(1−ρ̂)2>0.K''(\hat\rho)=\frac\rho{\hat\rho^2}+\frac{1-\rho}{(1-\hat\rho)^2}>0.

Thus this is the unique minimum, with KL equal to zero.

4 · Continue through averaging and an activation

Since ∂ρ̂/∂ai=1/B\partial\hat\rho/\partial a_i=1/B ,

∂K∂ai=1B[−ρρ̂+1−ρ1−ρ̂].\frac{\partial K}{\partial a_i}=\frac1B\left[-\frac\rho{\hat\rho}+\frac{1-\rho}{1-\hat\rho}\right].

If ai=σ(zi)a_i=\sigma(z_i) , multiply by ai(1−ai)a_i(1-a_i) to obtain ∂K/∂zi\partial K/\partial z_i . A total objective weighted by γ\gamma multiplies this contribution by γ\gamma . Every example contributes to the shared mean, so this regularizer couples observations in a batch.

5 · Do not apply the probability formula blindly

An unconstrained ReLU activation can exceed one, so its average is not automatically a Bernoulli probability. Use a compatible bounded activation/statistic or a different sparsity penalty. Clamping for numerical safety changes gradients at the clamped boundary and should be explicit.

Dropout’s stochastic mechanism

Let hh be a hidden activation vector and q=1−pq=1-p the keep probability, with 0<q≤10<q\le1 for the scaling formula below. In inverted dropout,

mj∼Bernoulli(q),h̃=m⊙hq.m_j\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q),\qquad\tilde h=\frac{m\odot h}{q}.

Conditioning on the original activation, 𝔼[h̃j∣hj]=hj\mathbb E[\tilde h_j\mid h_j]=h_j . Scaling during training therefore preserves the activation’s expectation, and the full activation is used without that random mask during ordinary evaluation.

The guide’s example takes h=(2,1,−3,4)h=(2,1,-3,4) , p=0.5p=0.5 , and mask (1,0,1,0)(1,0,1,0) . The result is (4,0,−6,0)(4,0,-6,0) . Dropping half the entries does not mean the surviving entries retain their original value under inverted scaling.

Step by step Derive dropout’s mean, variance, and a nonlinear counterexample

Setting. Treat an activation hh as fixed and draw m∼Bernoulli(q)m\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q) , with keep probability 0<q≤10<q\le1 . Inverted dropout returns h̃=mh/q\tilde h=mh/q . The case q=0q=0 is excluded from this analytic scaling formula.

𝔼[h̃]=qhq+(1−q)0=h.\mathbb E[\tilde h]=q\frac hq+(1-q)0=h.

𝔼[h̃2]=qh2q2=h2q,\mathbb E[\tilde h^2]=q\frac{h^2}{q^2}=\frac{h^2}{q},
Var(h̃)=𝔼[h̃2]−𝔼[h̃]2=1−qqh2.\operatorname{Var}(\tilde h)=\mathbb E[\tilde h^2]-\mathbb E[\tilde h]^2 =\frac{1-q}{q}h^2.

For independent masks mjm_j and s=∑jwjmjhj/qs=\sum_jw_jm_jh_j/q , linearity gives 𝔼[s]=∑jwjhj\mathbb E[s]=\sum_jw_jh_j . Independence makes cross covariances zero, so

Var(s)=1−qq∑jwj2hj2.\operatorname{Var}(s)=\frac{1-q}{q}\sum_jw_j^2h_j^2.

For squared loss and fixed target yy ,

𝔼[(s−y)2]=(𝔼[s]−y)2+Var(s).\mathbb E[(s-y)^2]=(\mathbb E[s]-y)^2+\operatorname{Var}(s).

In this particular linear-output case, dropout adds a feature-dependent quadratic penalty. It is not a universal single L2 coefficient for an entire nonlinear network.

4 · Show why the full deterministic network need not equal the stochastic mean

Take h=1h=1 , q=1/2q=1/2 , and a following transformation f(u)=ReLU(u−1)f(u)=\operatorname{ReLU}(u-1) . The stochastic outputs are f(0)=0f(0)=0 and f(2)=1f(2)=1 , so 𝔼[f(h̃)]=1/2\mathbb E[f(\tilde h)]=1/2 . But f(𝔼[h̃])=f(1)=0f(\mathbb E[\tilde h])=f(1)=0 . Preserving a layer’s activation mean does not allow expectation to pass through every later nonlinearity.

5 · Connect the identity to evaluation behavior

Ordinary inverted-dropout evaluation uses the unchanged activation hh instead of resampling and does not multiply it by qq again. This matches the layerwise scaling convention, not an exact integration over all possible nonlinear subnetworks.

Why it can regularize

The guide gives three complementary intuitions. Units cannot rely on the same partners always being present; this discourages co-adaptation. Many stochastic subnetworks share parameters during training. Multiplicative noise encourages robustness to missing internal features.

The expectation identity is local to the masked activation. A nonlinear downstream function generally does not commute with expectation. Treating dropout as a related-subnetwork ensemble is therefore an intuition, not an exact statement that one deterministic pass equals all-network averaging.

Dropout does not erase learned weights

After a masked training pass, the weights still exist. In ordinary evaluation the network uses all units. LASSO’s exact parameter zeros and a sparse code’s selective activation pattern are different from these temporary masks.

The probability pp is a hyperparameter, not a desired fraction of permanently removed parameters. Excessive dropout can create underfitting and noisy optimization. The PDF also cautions that placement depends on architecture: convolutional, recurrent, residual, and Transformer components have different conventions.

Read train and evaluation behavior explicitly

The official PyTorch MNIST example demonstrates dropout in a full classifier. The API reference documents the probability and scaling behavior. Use the model’s intended training/evaluation modes during metric calculation. A stochastic training result and a deterministic evaluation result are measuring different computations, so differences should be interpreted in that context.

Check your understanding Does dropout perform permanent feature selection?

No. It temporarily zeros activations during training and normally uses the full network at evaluation. LASSO can produce structurally zero parameters, while sparse coding penalizes the representation for each example. These are different objects and different meanings of sparsity.
