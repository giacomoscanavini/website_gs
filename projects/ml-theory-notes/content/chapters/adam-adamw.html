Adam, bias correction, and AdamW

Adam combines a moving average of gradients with adaptive coordinate scaling. Bias correction accounts for zero-initialized averages; AdamW applies weight decay separately from them.

Two memories serve two different purposes

Adam stores an exponential average of gradients and of squared gradients:

mt=β1mt−1+(1−β1)gt,m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t,
vt=β2vt−1+(1−β2)(gt⊙gt).v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)(g_t\odot g_t).

The first moment mtm_t smooths direction. The second moment vtv_t tracks a coordinate-wise magnitude scale. It is an average of squared gradients, not the centered variance unless the mean correction is also accounted for.

An intuitive comparison is: RMSProp uses the current gradient over a recent magnitude estimate; Adam uses a smoothed gradient over a recent magnitude estimate.

Why zero initialization needs a correction

Start with m0=v0=0m_0=v_0=0 . For a constant scalar gradient gg , expanding the recurrence gives

mt=(1−β1t)g.m_t=(1-\beta_1^t)g.

The moment estimate is initially pulled toward the zero starting value. The correction is

m̂t=mt1−β1t,v̂t=vt1−β2t.\hat m_t=\frac{m_t}{1-\beta_1^t},\qquad \hat v_t=\frac{v_t}{1-\beta_2^t}.

Under the constant-gradient calculation, the corrected moments recover gg and g2g^2 . “Bias correction” refers to this initialization effect in the moving averages, not correction for biased training data or statistical model bias.

The update is

θt+1=θt−ηm̂tv̂t+ϵ.\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}.

Here η\eta is a base scale; the normalized moment ratio also controls the step. Adam is not free of learning-rate tuning.

Step by step Derive both bias-correction factors and the first Adam step

Setting. Constant β1,β2∈[0,1)\beta_1,\beta_2\in[0,1) , m0=v0=0m_0=v_0=0 . First derive the moving-average algebra; then specify when it is an unbiased moment estimate.

1 · Unroll the first-moment recurrence

mt=(1−β1)gt+β1(1−β1)gt−1+⋯+(1−β1)β1t−1g1.m_t=(1-\beta_1)g_t+\beta_1(1-\beta_1)g_{t-1}+\cdots+(1-\beta_1)\beta_1^{t-1}g_1.

Equivalently mt=(1−β1)∑j=1tβ1t−jgjm_t=(1-\beta_1)\sum_{j=1}^t\beta_1^{t-j}g_j .

2 · Sum the weight mass

The geometric identity gives

(1−β1)∑k=0t−1β1k=1−β1t.(1-\beta_1)\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta_1^k=1-\beta_1^t.

If 𝔼[gj]=μ\mathbb E[g_j]=\mu for each included step, then 𝔼[mt]=(1−β1t)μ\mathbb E[m_t]=(1-\beta_1^t)\mu . Dividing by this weight mass gives m̂t=mt/(1−β1t)\hat m_t=m_t/(1-\beta_1^t) with expectation μ\mu . Independence across steps is not needed for this expectation; stationarity of the mean is.

3 · Repeat for squared gradients

With 𝔼[gj2]=s\mathbb E[g_j^2]=s constant,

vt=(1−β2)∑j=1tβ2t−jgj2,𝔼[vt]=(1−β2t)s.v_t=(1-\beta_2)\sum_{j=1}^t\beta_2^{t-j}g_j^2,\qquad \mathbb E[v_t]=(1-\beta_2^t)s.

Thus v̂t=vt/(1−β2t)\hat v_t=v_t/(1-\beta_2^t) removes the zero-start weight deficit.

4 · Work the first step numerically

For g1=0.4g_1=0.4 ,

m1=(1−0.9)(0.4)=0.04,m_1=(1-0.9)(0.4)=0.04,
v1=(1−0.999)(0.4)2=0.001(0.16)=0.00016.v_1=(1-0.999)(0.4)^2=0.001(0.16)=0.00016.

Then m̂1=0.04/0.1=0.4\hat m_1=0.04/0.1=0.4 and v̂1=0.00016/0.001=0.16\hat v_1=0.00016/0.001=0.16 . With η=0.001\eta=0.001 and ϵ=10−8\epsilon=10^{-8} ,

Δθ1=−0.0010.40.4+10−8≈−0.000999999975.\Delta\theta_1=-0.001\frac{0.4}{0.4+10^{-8}}\approx-0.000999999975.

5 · State exactly what is corrected

When moments change over time, the corrected quantity is a normalized history-weighted average, not necessarily an unbiased estimate of the current moment. Furthermore, m̂t/v̂t\hat m_t/\sqrt{\hat v_t} is a nonlinear ratio, so separately corrected moments do not imply an unbiased optimizer direction. No general convergence guarantee follows from these identities alone.

First-step calculation

Let g1=0.4g_1=0.4 , β1=0.9\beta_1=0.9 , β2=0.999\beta_2=0.999 , and η=0.001\eta=0.001 . With zero initial moments,

m1=0.04,v1=0.00016,m_1=0.04,\qquad v_1=0.00016,
m̂1=0.4,v̂1=0.16.\hat m_1=0.4,\qquad\hat v_1=0.16.

Ignoring a negligible ϵ\epsilon , the parameter displacement is

Δθ1=−0.0010.40.16=−0.001.\Delta\theta_1=-0.001\frac{0.4}{\sqrt{0.16}}=-0.001.

For a nonzero first gradient, the magnitude is close to the base rate. Later steps depend on history, so this observation is not a rule that Adam always moves by exactly η\eta.

L2 regularization versus weight decay

For the objective Jreg=J+λ2∥θ∥2J_{\mathrm{reg}}=J+\tfrac\lambda2\lVert\theta\rVert^2 , its gradient is g+λθg+\lambda\theta . With basic SGD,

θt+1=θt−η(gt+λθt)=(1−ηλ)θt−ηgt.\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta(g_t+\lambda\theta_t) =(1-\eta\lambda)\theta_t-\eta g_t.

This algebra gives the familiar equivalence between L2 and multiplicative decay for that basic update . If the combined gradient instead passes through adaptive moment calculations, the penalty contribution affects the moments and is rescaled coordinate by coordinate. It is no longer the same independent shrinkage.

AdamW applies decay separately from the adaptive gradient term:

θt+1=θt−ηm̂tv̂t+ϵ−ηλθt.\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}-\eta\lambda\theta_t.

The parameter shrinkage does not enter Adam’s first- or second-moment estimates. Implementation order and optimizer options still matter when reproducing an exact update.

Step by step Derive when L2 equals weight decay—and when it does not

1 · Differentiate the L2 penalty coordinate by coordinate

Ω(θ)=λ2∑jθj2,∂Ω∂θj=λθj.\Omega(\theta)=\frac\lambda2\sum_j\theta_j^2,\qquad \frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial\theta_j}=\lambda\theta_j.

Thus the total gradient is g+λθg+\lambda\theta .

2 · Substitute into plain SGD

θt+1=θt−η(gt+λθt)=(1−ηλ)θt⏟multiplicative term−ηgt.\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta(g_t+\lambda\theta_t) =\underbrace{(1-\eta\lambda)\theta_t}_{\text{multiplicative term}}-\eta g_t.

When 0<ηλ<10<\eta\lambda<1 , the multiplicative term is literal shrinkage toward zero. The equality is algebraic; unusually large decay steps need not behave as gentle shrinkage.

3 · Add a diagonal preconditioner

For a fixed positive diagonal matrix PtP_t , a coupled penalty gives

θt+1=(I−ηλPt)θt−ηPtgt.\theta_{t+1}=(I-\eta\lambda P_t)\theta_t-\eta P_tg_t.

Coordinate jj shrinks by 1−ηλPt,jj1-\eta\lambda P_{t,jj} , not by one common factor. Decoupled decay instead uses

θt+1=(1−ηλ)θt−ηPtgt.\theta_{t+1}=(1-\eta\lambda)\theta_t-\eta P_tg_t.

These are unequal unless the preconditioner and decay coefficients satisfy the required special relation.

4 · Identify Adam’s additional history effect

If gt+λθtg_t+\lambda\theta_t is passed into Adam, both mtm_t and vtv_t include that penalty contribution. In particular the square contains

(gt+λθt)2=gt2+2λgtθt+λ2θt2.(g_t+\lambda\theta_t)^2=g_t^2+2\lambda g_t\theta_t+\lambda^2\theta_t^2.

Decay changes future moments as well as the current step. AdamW updates moments from the data gradient and applies parameter decay separately.

5 · Do not extend the plain-SGD identity to momentum automatically

Coupled L2 with momentum uses vt=βvt−1+gt+λθtv_t=\beta v_{t-1}+g_t+\lambda\theta_t . Its buffer retains past penalty gradients . Decaying parameters outside that buffer is a different procedure. The simple exact equivalence in step 2 applies to basic SGD without momentum, not to every optimizer bearing the SGD name.

Interpreting hyperparameters and failure

β1\beta_1 controls direction memory. β2\beta_2 controls how quickly the magnitude estimate reacts. An extremely long memory can be stable but slow to follow a sudden change in gradient statistics. ϵ\epsilon stabilizes division and matters when moments are small. Weight decay controls a separate shrinkage effect.

Rapidly decreasing training loss does not establish good generalization. Evaluate learning rate, schedule, regularization, and batch size with a validation protocol. A useful Adam–SGD comparison concerns two tuned training procedures, not two optimizer names at one shared rate.

Check your understanding Why is adding L2 to Adam’s loss not generally the same as AdamW?

An L2 penalty adds λθ to the gradient before Adam’s moment estimation and adaptive normalization. Decoupled weight decay shrinks parameters separately from that gradient transformation. Basic SGD has an algebraic equivalence; adaptive methods generally do not.
