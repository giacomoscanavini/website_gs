The cycle has distinct stages

The source’s cycle is forward pass, loss, backward pass, and parameter update. The forward pass computes what the current model predicts. The loss translates prediction quality into a scalar. Backpropagation computes local sensitivities with respect to the parameters. The optimizer makes an update using those sensitivities and possibly stored history.

This division matters when diagnosing a failure. A wrong target shape is a problem with the objective being computed. A detached activation is a problem with derivative connectivity. A learning rate that is too large is a problem with update magnitude. Changing the optimizer will not repair all three.

Mini-batches are averages of objectives

For BB examples, the guide defines

LB(θ)=1B∑i∈BLi(θ),∇θLB=1B∑i∈B∇θLi.L_B(\theta)=\frac1B\sum_{i\in B}L_i(\theta),\qquad \nabla_\theta L_B=\frac1B\sum_{i\in B}\nabla_\theta L_i.

Linearity of differentiation gives the second equality. The batch gradient is not the gradient at the average input; for a nonlinear model, averaging inputs and then differentiating is a different calculation. We average the individual losses or gradients evaluated at the same parameters . Equating separately computed per-example or microbatch gradients to one full batch additionally assumes the forward computation is separable across observations. Training BatchNorm, for example, depends on the other batch members and can break that equivalence.

A batch tensor X∈ℝB×dX\in\mathbb R^{B\times d} stores BB observations with dd features. Parameters do not acquire a separate copy for each row. Matrix operations let many evaluations share the same weights efficiently.

Step by step Why unequal micro-batches need weighted gradient accumulation

Setting. Parameters stay fixed during accumulation. Each observation has a separable loss, with consistent reduction and no batch-dependent forward behavior. Partition NN examples into micro-batches CkC_k of sizes bkb_k , so N=∑kbkN=\sum_k b_k .

1 · Express the desired large-batch objective

L=1N∑i=1Nℓi=1N∑k∑i∈Ckℓi.L=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\ell_i=\frac1N\sum_k\sum_{i\in C_k}\ell_i.

Define the mean micro-batch loss Lk=bk−1∑i∈CkℓiL_k=b_k^{-1}\sum_{i\in C_k}\ell_i , hence ∑i∈Ckℓi=bkLk\sum_{i\in C_k}\ell_i=b_kL_k .

2 · Substitute and differentiate

L=∑kbkNLk,∇L=∑kbkN∇Lk.L=\sum_k\frac{b_k}{N}L_k,\qquad \nabla L=\sum_k\frac{b_k}{N}\nabla L_k.

A backward call on (bk/N)Lk(b_k/N)L_k adds exactly the required contribution when gradients accumulate. Step the optimizer once, after all contributions have been accumulated.

3 · See why an unweighted average can be wrong

For sizes b1=4b_1=4 and b2=1b_2=1 , the correct gradient is (4/5)∇L1+(1/5)∇L2(4/5)\nabla L_1+(1/5)\nabla L_2 . Using (∇L1+∇L2)/2(\nabla L_1+\nabla L_2)/2 gives the final observation weight 1/21/2 instead of 1/51/5 . Dividing by the number of micro-batches is correct only when their mean-loss denominators are equal.

4 · Account for output elements and masked tokens

For an elementwise mean over unequal numbers mkm_k of valid outputs, replace bkb_k by mkm_k . Padding, ignored labels, class-weighted means, and variable-length sequences can change the denominator. Weight by the actual quantity averaged, not automatically the number of input rows.

5 · State the equivalence boundaries

An optimizer step between micro-batches changes the parameters and breaks this algebra. BatchNorm using micro-batch statistics changes the forward function; dropout draws may also differ from a monolithic run. Even in the separable case, floating-point summation order can cause small differences. An explicit penalty added to every micro-batch must receive weights summing to one so it is not inadvertently counted multiple times.

Epochs, iterations, and data passes

With 60,000 observations and batches of 100, the guide counts 600 optimizer updates per epoch. Twenty epochs yield about 12,000 updates. An epoch measures exposure to data; an iteration measures an update. This distinction becomes important when comparing runs with different batch sizes: the same number of epochs can imply a different number of updates.

Changing from a sum to a mean loss also changes scale. If every per-example loss is identical, the summed gradient grows with batch size while the mean gradient does not. Penalties added outside that average must be scaled intentionally; otherwise changing batch size or reduction can change the effective amount of regularization.

Autograd implements the derivative graph

The computational graph records operations connecting parameters to the loss. In the simple source example z=wx+bz=wx+b and L=z2L=z^2 , the graph reuses the local derivatives 2z2z and xx to obtain 2zx2zx for ∂L/∂w\partial L/\partial w . A large model applies the same principle to many operations.

The official autograd tutorial provides a concrete graph, differentiation examples, and a demonstration of turning gradient tracking off. The purpose of that link is to connect the derivative you can derive by hand with the framework’s execution—not to make tensor-method memorization the center of the chapter.

Step by step Derive reverse-mode accumulation on a branching graph

1 · Make every intermediate operation explicit

Take a scalar example

u=wx,v=u+b,r=u2,L=v2+r.u=wx,\qquad v=u+b,\qquad r=u^2,\qquad L=v^2+r.

The intermediate uu feeds both vv and rr . Its influence cannot be captured by following only one branch.

2 · Start with the scalar objective

Define an adjoint a‾=∂L/∂a\bar a=\partial L/\partial a . Initially L‾=1\bar L=1 . The last addition sends one unit to each summand. Differentiating v2v^2 gives v‾=2v\bar v=2v ; differentiating the linear contribution rr gives r‾=1\bar r=1 .

3 · Add all contributions entering the shared variable

The route through vv contributes v‾∂v/∂u=2v\bar v\,\partial v/\partial u=2v . The route through rr contributes r‾∂r/∂u=2u\bar r\,\partial r/\partial u=2u . Therefore

u‾=2v+2u.\bar u=2v+2u.

This addition is the reverse-mode rule at a branch junction.

4 · Propagate to parameters and input

∂L∂w=u‾x=(2v+2u)x,\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}=\bar u\,x=(2v+2u)x,
∂L∂b=v‾=2v,∂L∂x=u‾w.\frac{\partial L}{\partial b}=\bar v=2v,\qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial x}=\bar u\,w.

Check by eliminating intermediates: L=(wx+b)2+(wx)2L=(wx+b)^2+(wx)^2 . Direct differentiation gives 2(wx+b)x+2(wx)x2(wx+b)x+2(wx)x for the weight, the same expression.

5 · Generalize the rule

For a graph node aa with immediate downstream nodes cc , the total sensitivity is

a‾=∑c:a→cc‾∂c∂a.\bar a=\sum_{c:\,a\to c}\bar c\frac{\partial c}{\partial a}.

Process nodes in reverse dependency order, using forward-pass values to evaluate the local derivatives. Reusing the accumulated adjoint is why reverse mode does not require one complete loss reevaluation per parameter.

Implementation distinctions worth knowing

The following are official PyTorch implementation notes , supplementing the PDFs. Gradients normally accumulate in parameter .grad fields. For ordinary independent mini-batch updates, the training loop clears old gradients, computes the new loss, calls .backward() , and then calls .step() .

model.train() and model.eval() select module behavior such as dropout’s active/inactive state. They do not themselves enable or disable autograd. Conversely, no_grad() changes graph recording, not dropout’s train/evaluation mode. Validation generally needs both the intended evaluation behavior and no unnecessary gradient recording.

The same separation helps with checkpointing. A checkpoint of parameters is a saved model state. An optimizer can also have state—momentum or moment estimates—that influences the next update. These are distinct objects even though both participate in training.

Reading learning curves

Compare training and validation quantities measured consistently. Training can include random augmentation, dropout, or explicit penalties that validation does not. A raw difference between two differently defined losses does not by itself diagnose overfitting. The guides’ essential distinction is whether poor behavior reflects optimization, insufficient representation, or generalization; the diagnosis chapter develops that interpretation.

Check your understanding Is one epoch one optimizer update?

Usually not. An epoch is one traversal of the training data. With N examples and batch size B, there are approximately N/B updates per epoch, subject to the final partial batch and the chosen accumulation procedure.
