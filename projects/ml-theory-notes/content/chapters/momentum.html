The narrow-valley problem

Suppose the objective changes rapidly in one direction and slowly in another. A gradient step can jump across the steep direction while moving only a little along the shallow one. The next gradient then points back across the valley. Plain descent can spend many steps repeating this correction.

Momentum stores a velocity-like summary of previous gradients. In the optimization PDF’s convention,

vt=βvt−1+gt,θt+1=θt−ηvt.v_t=\beta v_{t-1}+g_t,\qquad \theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta v_t.

Expanding the recursion shows what is retained:

vt=gt+βgt−1+β2gt−2+⋯.v_t=g_t+\beta g_{t-1}+\beta^2g_{t-2}+\cdots.

Recent gradients count most; old contributions decay geometrically. If a coordinate’s gradients repeatedly agree, their contributions accumulate. If they alternate signs, they partly cancel.

Work through the source’s two updates

Set η=0.1\eta=0.1 , β=0.9\beta=0.9 , and v0=0v_0=0 . For g1=(2,1)⊤g_1=(2,1)^\top ,

v1=[21],Δθ1=[−0.2−0.1].v_1=\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\end{bmatrix},\qquad \Delta\theta_1=\begin{bmatrix}-0.2\\-0.1\end{bmatrix}.

Now let g2=(1.5,−0.8)⊤g_2=(1.5,-0.8)^\top . Then

v2=0.9[21]+[1.5−0.8]=[3.30.1].v_2=0.9\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}1.5\\-0.8\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}3.3\\0.1\end{bmatrix}.

The first coordinate retains accumulated motion, while the second nearly cancels. The new displacement is (−0.33,−0.01)⊤(-0.33,-0.01)^\top . This numerical example explains the mechanism more directly than the phrase “a ball rolling downhill.”

Memory and overshoot

The guide describes an approximate memory timescale of 1/(1−β)1/(1-\beta) . A value of 0.90.9 corresponds to roughly a ten-step scale; 0.990.99 keeps history much longer. This is a characteristic decay scale, not a hard cutoff.

Longer memory can stabilize a persistent direction but makes the optimizer slower to respond when the useful direction changes. Momentum can carry a parameter beyond a minimum or into a region where earlier gradients are no longer relevant. The rate and momentum coefficient should therefore be considered together.

Step by step Unroll momentum and derive its effective memory

Setting. vt=βvt−1+gtv_t=\beta v_{t-1}+g_t , v0=0v_0=0 , and constant 0≤β<10\le\beta<1 . This is the unnormalized convention from the optimization guide.

1 · Expand the first three recurrences

v1=g1,v2=βg1+g2,v_1=g_1,\qquad v_2=\beta g_1+g_2,
v3=β(βg1+g2)+g3=β2g1+βg2+g3.v_3=\beta(\beta g_1+g_2)+g_3=\beta^2g_1+\beta g_2+g_3.

Each substitution moves every older gradient one power of β\beta further into the past. Induction therefore gives

vt=∑j=1tβt−jgj.v_t=\sum_{j=1}^t\beta^{t-j}g_j.

For nonzero initialization, add the term βtv0\beta^t v_0 .

2 · Evaluate a constant gradient

If gj=gg_j=g for every step, use the finite geometric sum

1+β+⋯+βt−1=1−βt1−β1+\beta+\cdots+\beta^{t-1}=\frac{1-\beta^t}{1-\beta}

to obtain vt=g(1−βt)/(1−β)v_t=g(1-\beta^t)/(1-\beta) . As t→∞t\to\infty , the buffer approaches g/(1−β)g/(1-\beta) . Thus the long-run displacement is approximately −ηg/(1−β)-\eta g/(1-\beta) , not −ηg-\eta g .

3 · Translate the normalized convention

If v‾t=βv‾t−1+(1−β)gt\bar v_t=\beta\bar v_{t-1}+(1-\beta)g_t with zero initialization, the expansion gives v‾t=(1−β)vt\bar v_t=(1-\beta)v_t . To obtain the same displacement, η‾v‾t=ηvt\bar\eta\bar v_t=\eta v_t requires η‾=η/(1−β)\bar\eta=\eta/(1-\beta) .

4 · Derive a decay timescale

A contribution kk steps old is multiplied by βk\beta^k . Set βτ=e−1\beta^\tau=e^{-1} and take logarithms:

τlogβ=−1,τ=−1logβ.\tau\log\beta=-1,\qquad\tau=-\frac1{\log\beta}.

For β\beta near one, write β=1−u\beta=1-u and use log(1−u)≈−u\log(1-u)\approx-u , giving τ≈1/(1−β)\tau\approx1/(1-\beta) . This is an approximation; for β=0.9\beta=0.9 the exact e-fold time is about 9.499.49 steps. The half-life is log(1/2)/logβ\log(1/2)/\log\beta , about 6.586.58 steps at 0.90.9 .

Two conventions in the supplied PDFs

The basics PDF writes an exponentially averaged gradient,

v‾t=βv‾t−1+(1−β)gt,\bar v_t=\beta\bar v_{t-1}+(1-\beta)g_t,

while the dedicated optimization PDF uses the unnormalized sum shown above. These equations express the same history-weighting idea but not the same numerical scale at a fixed learning rate . With zero initialization and constant β\beta , v‾t=(1−β)vt\bar v_t=(1-\beta)v_t ; matching parameter trajectories requires a corresponding step-size adjustment.

Both source conventions are retained here rather than silently blended. In actual PyTorch code, consult the SGD reference for momentum-buffer initialization, dampening, and its precise Nesterov update. The examples in one convention should not be compared numerically to another without translation.

Nesterov’s look-ahead intuition

The optimization guide writes a look-ahead parameter point and gradient:

θ̃t=θt−ηβvt−1,\tilde\theta_t=\theta_t-\eta\beta v_{t-1},
vt=βvt−1+∇J(θ̃t),θt+1=θt−ηvt.v_t=\beta v_{t-1}+\nabla J(\tilde\theta_t),\qquad \theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta v_t.

The idea is to evaluate the correction where existing momentum is about to take the parameters, rather than only where they are now. This anticipates part of the overshoot. Practical implementations may express the update in algebraically rearranged variables; the linked official algorithm is the appropriate reference when matching code exactly.

Step by step Connect look-ahead Nesterov to its rearranged update

Setting. Use constant learning rate η\eta , constant momentum β\beta , no dampening or weight decay, and an initial zero buffer. Different rates or initialization rules require redoing the variable transformation.

1 · Start with the look-ahead equations

yt=θt−ηβvt−1,gt=∇J(yt),y_t=\theta_t-\eta\beta v_{t-1},\qquad g_t=\nabla J(y_t),
vt=βvt−1+gt,θt+1=θt−ηvt.v_t=\beta v_{t-1}+g_t,\qquad \theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta v_t.

The gradient is evaluated at yty_t , not at θt\theta_t .

2 · Express next step’s look-ahead point

yt+1=θt+1−ηβvt=θt−η(1+β)vt.y_{t+1}=\theta_{t+1}-\eta\beta v_t=\theta_t-\eta(1+\beta)v_t.

From the first equation, θt=yt+ηβvt−1\theta_t=y_t+\eta\beta v_{t-1} . Substitute it:

yt+1=yt+ηβvt−1−η(1+β)vt.y_{t+1}=y_t+\eta\beta v_{t-1}-\eta(1+\beta)v_t.

3 · Eliminate the old buffer

The buffer recurrence implies βvt−1=vt−gt\beta v_{t-1}=v_t-g_t . Hence

yt+1=yt+η(vt−gt)−η(1+β)vt=yt−η(gt+βvt).y_{t+1}=y_t+\eta(v_t-g_t)-\eta(1+\beta)v_t=y_t-\eta(g_t+\beta v_t).

4 · Read the implemented form

If the stored parameter is yty_t , an implementation can compute its gradient, update the buffer, and step using gt+βvtg_t+\beta v_t without explicitly moving to a separate look-ahead point first. This explains the apparently different formula in a Nesterov SGD implementation.

5 · Preserve the interpretation of the variables

The sequences called yty_t and θt\theta_t are not identical. Comparing an implementation’s stored parameters against the wrong textbook variable makes two equivalent descriptions appear inconsistent. The official SGD algorithm includes additional choices such as initialization and dampening, so verify those before claiming numerical equivalence.

The distinction from adaptive optimizers

Momentum changes the direction estimate using history. By itself it does not construct a different normalization for every coordinate. AdaGrad and RMSProp use squared-gradient history for scale; Adam combines a smoothed direction with adaptive scaling. Keeping direction and scale separate makes the optimizer family easier to remember.

Check your understanding How does momentum help in a narrow valley?

Gradients that alternate sign across the steep direction partly cancel in the velocity, while gradients that persist along the shallow direction accumulate. Momentum can therefore reduce zig-zagging and accelerate progress, but it can also overshoot.
