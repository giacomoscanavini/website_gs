The prediction domain is part of the task

A network’s last layer must express the quantity being predicted. The guide distinguishes unrestricted regression, binary probabilities, mutually exclusive classes, and multiple independent labels. The number of outputs alone does not determine the interpretation.

For unrestricted regression, a final affine map can produce any real value. For binary classification, one logit parameterizes one Bernoulli probability. For multiclass classification, KK logits parameterize a distribution over KK mutually exclusive alternatives. In multilabel classification, KK sigmoid probabilities represent KK separate binary targets rather than one probability vector summing to one.

Squared error and its gradient

The source uses the convenient single-observation convention

L=12(ŷ−y)2,∂L∂ŷ=ŷ−y.L=\frac12(\hat y-y)^2,\qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial\hat y}=\hat y-y.

A positive residual gives a positive derivative, so a negative-gradient update tries to reduce the prediction; a negative residual reverses the direction. Large residuals contribute disproportionately because the loss is quadratic.

Implementation convention. PyTorch’s ordinary mean squared error omits the factor 1/21/2 and, by default, averages over elements. The derivative’s scale therefore differs from the source’s scalar convention. Neither convention is wrong, but a numerical derivation and its code must use the same normalization. Scaling the loss changes gradient scale and can require changes to the learning rate or penalty coefficient.

An unrestricted regression output should not automatically inherit the hidden activation. The guide’s signed-return example illustrates the issue: a ReLU output could never predict a negative value.

Step by step From Gaussian likelihood to squared loss and the conditional mean

1 · Specify an observation distribution

Assume Y∣X=x∼𝒩(fθ(x),σ2)Y\mid X=x\sim\mathcal N(f_\theta(x),\sigma^2) with fixed positive variance. For one target yy , the negative log-density is

−logpθ(y∣x)=12log(2πσ2)+(y−fθ(x))22σ2.-\log p_\theta(y\mid x)=\frac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)+\frac{(y-f_\theta(x))^2}{2\sigma^2}.

The log normalizing constant is independent of the predicted mean. For optimizing the mean, dropping that constant and multiplying by σ2\sigma^2 gives L=12(ŷ−y)2L=\tfrac12(\hat y-y)^2 .

2 · Differentiate the residual explicitly

Set r=ŷ−yr=\hat y-y . With the target fixed, dr/dŷ=1dr/d\hat y=1 . Hence

dLdŷ=12⋅2r⋅1=ŷ−y,d2Ldŷ2=1.\frac{dL}{d\hat y}=\frac12\cdot2r\cdot1=\hat y-y,\qquad \frac{d^2L}{d\hat y^2}=1.

For the mean of BB scalar examples, each prediction derivative is (ŷi−yi)/B(\hat y_i-y_i)/B . PyTorch’s unhalved MSE mean over MM output elements instead gives 2(ŷi−yi)/M2(\hat y_i-y_i)/M .

3 · Ask which prediction minimizes expected squared error

At a fixed xx , let m(x)=𝔼[Y∣X=x]m(x)=\mathbb E[Y\mid X=x] and let aa be a candidate scalar prediction. Add and subtract mm :

(Y−a)2=[(Y−m)+(m−a)]2.(Y-a)^2=[(Y-m)+(m-a)]^2.

Expanding gives (Y−m)2+2(Y−m)(m−a)+(m−a)2(Y-m)^2+2(Y-m)(m-a)+(m-a)^2 .

4 · Take the conditional expectation term by term

Because m−am-a is constant given xx and 𝔼[Y−m∣x]=0\mathbb E[Y-m\mid x]=0 ,

𝔼[(Y−a)2∣x]=Var(Y∣x)+(m−a)2.\mathbb E[(Y-a)^2\mid x]=\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid x)+(m-a)^2.

The first term cannot be changed by aa . The second is nonnegative and is zero only at a=ma=m . Thus squared-loss prediction targets a conditional mean, provided the required second moment is finite; a Gaussian model is not needed for this final conclusion.

5 · Preserve the qualification when learning variance

If σ2\sigma^2 is itself predicted, 12logσ2\tfrac12\log\sigma^2 is not constant and cannot be dropped. Minimizing only squared residual divided by a trainable variance would permit inflating the variance rather than learning a valid Gaussian likelihood.

Binary cross entropy and logits

With p=σ(z)p=\sigma(z) and y∈{0,1}y\in\{0,1\} ,

L=−ylogp−(1−y)log(1−p).L=-y\log p-(1-y)\log(1-p).

The target selects the negative log probability of the observed outcome. A confidently wrong prediction is penalized much more than a mildly uncertain one. This is why accuracy and cross entropy answer different questions.

Combining the derivatives of cross entropy and sigmoid gives

∂L∂z=p−y.\frac{\partial L}{\partial z}=p-y.

The complicated-looking terms cancel. For the running example, p≈0.363547p\approx0.363547 and y=1y=1 , so the output-logit derivative is approximately −0.636453-0.636453 . It asks the optimizer to increase the logit along an appropriate parameter direction.

The official BCEWithLogitsLoss reference documents a numerically stable fused calculation. The model returns zz ; the loss handles the sigmoid/logarithm combination. At inference, applying sigmoid gives pp . The loss implementation uses logarithmic identities to avoid explicitly forming unstable probabilities at extreme logits.

Step by step Derive binary cross entropy, its logit gradient, and its stable form

Setting. One observation, y∈{0,1}y\in\{0,1\} , p=σ(z)p=\sigma(z) , no class weighting, and no batch reduction yet.

1 · Write the Bernoulli probability

P(Y=y∣z)=py(1−p)1−y.P(Y=y\mid z)=p^y(1-p)^{1-y}.

For y=1y=1 this equals pp ; for y=0y=0 it equals 1−p1-p . The negative log is

L=−ylogp−(1−y)log(1−p).L=-y\log p-(1-y)\log(1-p).

2 · Differentiate with respect to probability

∂L∂p=−yp−(1−y)−11−p=−yp+1−y1−p.\frac{\partial L}{\partial p}=-\frac yp-(1-y)\frac{-1}{1-p}=-\frac yp+\frac{1-y}{1-p}.

Put both fractions over the same denominator:

−yp+1−y1−p=−y(1−p)+(1−y)pp(1−p)=p−yp(1−p).-\frac yp+\frac{1-y}{1-p}=\frac{-y(1-p)+(1-y)p}{p(1-p)}=\frac{p-y}{p(1-p)}.

3 · Apply the sigmoid derivative

Since dp/dz=p(1−p)dp/dz=p(1-p) , the chain rule gives

∂L∂z=p−yp(1−p)p(1−p)=p−y.\frac{\partial L}{\partial z}=\frac{p-y}{p(1-p)}p(1-p)=p-y.

For finite zz , 0<p<10<p<1 , so this cancellation is valid. Its stable software evaluation need not form the individually large intermediate fractions. The second derivative is p(1−p)≥0p(1-p)\ge0 , showing convexity in the logit . It does not make the loss convex in all deep-network weights.

4 · Eliminate probabilities from the loss

Use logp=z−log(1+ez)\log p=z-\log(1+e^z) and log(1−p)=−log(1+ez)\log(1-p)=-\log(1+e^z) . Substitution gives

L=−y[z−log(1+ez)]+(1−y)log(1+ez)=log(1+ez)−yz.L=-y[z-\log(1+e^z)]+(1-y)\log(1+e^z)=\log(1+e^z)-yz.

5 · Avoid exponentiating a large positive number

For z≥0z\ge0 , log(1+ez)=z+log(1+e−z)\log(1+e^z)=z+\log(1+e^{-z}) . For z<0z<0 , retain log(1+ez)\log(1+e^z) . Both branches combine as

L=max(z,0)−yz+log(1+e−|z|).L=\max(z,0)-yz+\log(1+e^{-|z|}).

The exponential argument is now nonpositive. This is the mathematical identity behind a stable logit-based calculation, not a promise about one particular kernel’s internal instruction sequence.

6 · Account for weighting and reduction

A mean over BB scalar losses divides the gradient by BB . With positive-class weight ww in −wylogp−(1−y)log(1−p)-wy\log p-(1-y)\log(1-p) , the unscaled gradient becomes

wy(p−1)+(1−y)p.w y(p-1)+(1-y)p.

It reduces to p−yp-y only when w=1w=1 . Reusing the unweighted gradient for a weighted loss is incorrect.

Multiclass cross entropy

Softmax defines

pk=exp(zk)∑j=1Kexp(zj),∑kpk=1.p_k=\frac{\exp(z_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\exp(z_j)},\qquad \sum_kp_k=1.

For true class cc , the loss is −logpc-\log p_c . With a one-hot target yy , the equivalent expression is −∑kyklogpk-\sum_k y_k\log p_k . The gradient with respect to each logit is

∂L∂zk=pk−yk.\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_k}=p_k-y_k.

The scalar binary residual is therefore part of a broader pattern: under the standard cross-entropy pairing, the logit gradient is predicted probability minus target probability. This last expression is an expanded derivation of the guide’s output and cross-entropy discussion.

Official API supplement. The usual PyTorch contract uses logits shaped [B, K] and integer class indices shaped [B] . The PDF describes this usual case. Current documentation also supports suitable probability targets, which is a separate API mode; a one-hot vector is not mandatory for ordinary classification.

Step by step Derive the softmax Jacobian and cross-entropy gradient

Setting. KK logits zjz_j , S=∑jezjS=\sum_j e^{z_j} , pi=ezi/Sp_i=e^{z_i}/S , and a fixed target distribution yi≥0y_i\ge0 with ∑iyi=1\sum_i y_i=1 . No class weighting is applied.

1 · Differentiate the denominator

For a chosen coordinate zjz_j , only one term in the sum varies, so ∂S/∂zj=ezj\partial S/\partial z_j=e^{z_j} . The numerator derivative is ∂ezi/∂zj=δijezi\partial e^{z_i}/\partial z_j=\delta_{ij}e^{z_i} , where δij\delta_{ij} is one if i=ji=j and zero otherwise.

2 · Use the quotient rule

∂pi∂zj=δijeziS−eziezjS2=pi(δij−pj).\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}=\frac{\delta_{ij}e^{z_i}S-e^{z_i}e^{z_j}}{S^2}=p_i(\delta_{ij}-p_j).

Diagonal entries are pi(1−pi)p_i(1-p_i) ; off-diagonal entries are −pipj-p_ip_j . In matrix form,

Jsoftmax=diag(p)−pp⊤.J_{\mathrm{softmax}}=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top.

Increasing one score necessarily changes other probabilities because their sum stays one.

3 · Differentiate cross entropy

L=−∑iyilogpi,∂L∂zj=−∑iyipi∂pi∂zj.L=-\sum_i y_i\log p_i,\qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial z_j}=-\sum_i\frac{y_i}{p_i}\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}.

Substitute the Jacobian entry and cancel pip_i :

∂L∂zj=−∑iyi(δij−pj)=−yj+pj∑iyi=pj−yj.\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_j}=-\sum_i y_i(\delta_{ij}-p_j)=-y_j+p_j\sum_i y_i=p_j-y_j.

A one-hot target is a special case; the algebra also works for a normalized soft target.

4 · Derive the log-sum-exp expression

Because logpi=zi−logS\log p_i=z_i-\log S ,

L=−∑iyizi+logS∑iyi=log∑jezj−y⊤z.L=-\sum_i y_i z_i+\log S\sum_i y_i=\log\sum_j e^{z_j}-y^\top z.

For a true class cc , y⊤z=zcy^\top z=z_c and L=log∑jezj−zcL=\log\sum_j e^{z_j}-z_c .

5 · Subtract a common maximum safely

For a=maxjzja=\max_j z_j ,

log∑jezj=a+log∑jezj−a.\log\sum_j e^{z_j}=a+\log\sum_j e^{z_j-a}.

This is an exact identity. It changes neither the probabilities nor the loss and avoids overflow from exponentiating large logits. The softmax gradient sums to zero: ∑j(pj−yj)=1−1=0\sum_j(p_j-y_j)=1-1=0 .

6 · State the weighted exception

For L=−∑iwiyilogpiL=-\sum_i w_i y_i\log p_i , the unreduced gradient is

∂L∂zj=pj∑iwiyi−wjyj.\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_j}=p_j\sum_iw_i y_i-w_jy_j.

The normalizing denominator of a library’s weighted mean is an additional choice. The plain p−yp-y result assumes unweighted targets of total mass one and the stated reduction.

Check your understanding Why should you usually not apply softmax before CrossEntropyLoss?

The class-index form of CrossEntropyLoss consumes logits and performs the log-softmax calculation internally. Applying softmax first changes the input from logits to probabilities and then applies a second normalization inside the loss. Apply softmax for interpreting predictions, not as a required preprocessing step for that loss.
