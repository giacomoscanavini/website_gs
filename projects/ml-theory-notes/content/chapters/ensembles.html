Average predictions, not explanations

For MM component predictions, simple averaging is ŷ=M−1∑mŷm\hat y=M^{-1}\sum_m\hat y_m . Weighted averaging uses

ŷ=∑mwmŷm,∑mwm=1.\hat y=\sum_mw_m\hat y_m,\qquad\sum_mw_m=1.

The guide describes choosing weights on validation or out-of-fold predictions, sometimes with nonnegativity constraints to improve stability. Weights are part of model selection, not an opportunity to use the final test set for one more optimization.

Combining models of similar quality can help when their errors differ. Combining two nearly identical errors usually adds little. Combining a very weak predictor can also hurt, even if it is different.

Covariance determines the variance of the combination

Let the prediction-error covariance matrix be Σ\Sigma . For unbiased estimators and weights summing to one, ensemble variance is

Var(eens)=w⊤Σw.\operatorname{Var}(e_{\mathrm{ens}})=w^\top\Sigma w.

To minimize that variance, form a Lagrangian for minww⊤Σw\min_w w^\top\Sigma w subject to 𝟏⊤w=1\mathbf1^\top w=1 . The first-order condition gives 2Σw−ν𝟏=02\Sigma w-\nu\mathbf1=0 . If Σ\Sigma is invertible, normalization yields the source’s result:

w*=Σ−1𝟏𝟏⊤Σ−1𝟏.w^*=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1}{\mathbf1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1}.

This formula shows why standalone accuracy is not enough. A model’s contribution depends on how its errors covary with the others.

Step by step Derive covariance-optimal weights and include bias in the objective

Setting. Let model errors e∈ℝMe\in\mathbb R^M be unbiased with positive-definite covariance Σ\Sigma . Require 𝟏⊤w=1\mathbf1^\top w=1 so a common target level is preserved. Initially allow negative weights.

1 · Write the ensemble variance

The combined error is w⊤ew^\top e . Since 𝔼[e]=0\mathbb E[e]=0 ,

𝔼[(w⊤e)2]=𝔼[w⊤ee⊤w]=w⊤𝔼[ee⊤]w=w⊤Σw.\mathbb E[(w^\top e)^2]=\mathbb E[w^\top ee^\top w] =w^\top\mathbb E[ee^\top]w=w^\top\Sigma w.

2 · Introduce the equality constraint

Use the Lagrangian

ℒ(w,ν)=w⊤Σw−ν(𝟏⊤w−1).\mathcal L(w,\nu)=w^\top\Sigma w-\nu(\mathbf1^\top w-1).

For symmetric Σ\Sigma , entrywise differentiation of ∑ijwiΣijwj\sum_{ij}w_i\Sigma_{ij}w_j gives 2Σw2\Sigma w , so stationarity requires 2Σw−ν𝟏=02\Sigma w-\nu\mathbf1=0 .

3 · Solve for the multiplier

Because Σ\Sigma is invertible,

w=ν2Σ−1𝟏.w=\frac\nu2\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1.

Substitute into 𝟏⊤w=1\mathbf1^\top w=1 :

ν2𝟏⊤Σ−1𝟏=1⇒ν2=1𝟏⊤Σ−1𝟏.\frac\nu2\mathbf1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1=1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac\nu2=\frac1{\mathbf1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1}.

Hence

w*=Σ−1𝟏𝟏⊤Σ−1𝟏.w^*=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1}{\mathbf1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1}.

Positive definiteness makes the quadratic strictly convex, so this feasible stationary point is the unique optimum.

4 · Obtain the minimum value

Put c=𝟏⊤Σ−1𝟏c=\mathbf1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1 . Then

w*⊤Σw*=𝟏⊤Σ−1ΣΣ−1𝟏c2=cc2=1c.w^{*\top}\Sigma w^*=\frac{\mathbf1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1}{c^2}=\frac c{c^2}=\frac1c.

This quantifies why error covariance, rather than just each marginal variance, matters.

5 · Identify changes that invalidate the simple formula

If errors have mean vector bb , then 𝔼[ee⊤]=Σ+bb⊤\mathbb E[ee^\top]=\Sigma+bb^\top and

𝔼[(w⊤e)2]=w⊤Σw+(w⊤b)2.\mathbb E[(w^\top e)^2]=w^\top\Sigma w+(w^\top b)^2.

Minimum variance is no longer identical to minimum squared error. Nonnegativity constraints wm≥0w_m\ge0 introduce inequality constraints; singular covariance removes the inverse; estimation error can produce unstable weights. These require a modified optimization problem, regularization, or simpler weights—not blind substitution into the displayed formula.

Assumptions and practical limitations

The expression above ignores nonnegativity constraints and assumes unbiased estimators and a usable covariance estimate. It can produce negative weights. Highly correlated predictions can make the covariance estimate ill-conditioned and the weights unstable. These limitations follow from the stated quadratic problem; the formula is not a blanket recommendation to invert an empirical covariance matrix and trust the result.

If the component errors have different biases, minimizing variance alone is not the same as minimizing total prediction error. A robust practical combination can therefore prefer constrained or simple weights, selected using the intended validation protocol.

Stacking learns a combination function

Stacking trains a meta-model

ŷ=g(ŷ1,…,ŷM).\hat y=g(\hat y_1,\ldots,\hat y_M).

The base predictions act as input features. The critical data rule is that meta-model training predictions must be out-of-fold or otherwise out of sample for each base model. A base learner’s training predictions can be overly optimistic and can leak its fitted knowledge into the meta-level training process.

A useful expanded procedural view is: fit base models inside each training fold, predict the held-out fold, assemble those held-out predictions, and fit the meta-model on that assembled matrix. For time-dependent data, the fold design must also respect chronology. Refitting base models for deployment is a subsequent step, not a reason to replace the out-of-sample meta-training inputs with in-sample ones.

Bagging versus boosting

The guide contrasts largely independent fitting plus averaging in bagging with sequential error correction in boosting. Later boosted learners focus on residual mistakes of earlier learners. This can address bias as well as variance, whereas the canonical bagging motivation is variance reduction.

These are different constructions. An average of independently initialized neural networks is an ensemble but not automatically classical bootstrap bagging. A meta-model over their predictions is stacking. Naming the actual source of diversity and the actual combination rule is more informative than calling all of them “ensemble learning.”

Step by step Why squared-error boosting fits residuals

Setting. Fix training points and regard the current fitted values Fi=F(xi)F_i=F(x_i) as coordinates of a vector. Use empirical loss J(F)=∑i(yi−Fi)2/(2n)J(F)=\sum_i(y_i-F_i)^2/(2n) . A new weak learner hh will provide a structured direction in this prediction space.

1 · Differentiate with respect to a fitted value

∂J∂Fi=12n2(yi−Fi)(−1)=Fi−yin.\frac{\partial J}{\partial F_i}=\frac1{2n}\,2(y_i-F_i)(-1)=\frac{F_i-y_i}{n}.

The negative gradient is (yi−Fi)/n(y_i-F_i)/n . Apart from the shared constant 1/n1/n , it is the residual ri=yi−Fir_i=y_i-F_i .

2 · Approximate that direction with a learner

A weak learner cannot choose arbitrary values independently at every training point. Fitting it to targets rir_i approximates the negative-gradient direction using its restricted function family, for example shallow trees.

3 · Derive the best step along a fixed fitted learner

Let hi=h(xi)h_i=h(x_i) and consider Fi+γhiF_i+\gamma h_i . Then

J(γ)=12n∑i(ri−γhi)2,J(\gamma)=\frac1{2n}\sum_i(r_i-\gamma h_i)^2,
J′(γ)=−1n∑ihi(ri−γhi).J'(\gamma)=-\frac1n\sum_i h_i(r_i-\gamma h_i).

Set this to zero. If ∑ihi2>0\sum_i h_i^2>0 , the minimizing step is

γ*=∑ihiri∑ihi2.\gamma^*=\frac{\sum_i h_ir_i}{\sum_i h_i^2}.

A learning-rate factor 0<η≤10<\eta\le1 may shrink this to the update F←F+ηγ*hF\leftarrow F+\eta\gamma^*h .

4 · Separate this from bagging

The new residuals depend on previous learners, so boosting is sequential. Bagging fits perturbed versions of a base problem and averages predictions instead. For other differentiable losses, the pseudo-residual is the negative derivative of that loss, not necessarily yi−Fiy_i-F_i .

Check your understanding Why must stacking use out-of-fold base predictions?

Predictions made on the same rows used to fit a base learner can encode its training overfit. A meta-model trained on those predictions sees an unrealistically favorable relationship. Out-of-fold or otherwise out-of-sample predictions make its training inputs closer to the base learners’ deployment behavior.
