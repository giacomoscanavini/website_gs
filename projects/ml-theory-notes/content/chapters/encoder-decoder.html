Encoders, decoders, and teacher forcing

An encoder constructs a representation; a decoder turns it into an output. Autoregressive decoding conditions each prediction on earlier outputs, with different available histories during training and inference.

Encoder and decoder describe roles

An encoder transforms an input into a useful representation. A decoder transforms a representation into an output. Either can use recurrent layers, attention, convolutions, or graph computations. The label specifies information flow, not a particular neuron or layer.

In classical recurrent sequence-to-sequence modeling, the encoder processes x1:Tx_{1:T} into context cc . The decoder models

p(y1:U∣x1:T)=∏u=1Up(yu∣y<u,c).p(y_{1:U}\mid x_{1:T})=\prod_{u=1}^{U}p(y_u\mid y_{<u},c).

The input and output lengths can differ. At each output step, the model conditions on source information and earlier target tokens. This is different from requiring one output aligned with every input position.

Why the autoregressive factorization is useful

Rather than produce a whole structured sequence as an unstructured object, the model makes a series of conditional predictions. For observed target sequence y*y^* , the usual token-level negative log-likelihood is

L=−∑u=1Ulogp(yu*∣y<u*,c).L=-\sum_{u=1}^{U}\log p(y_u^*\mid y_{<u}^*,c).

The model is trained to assign high probability to each observed token under the conditioning history. The chosen representation, decoder state, and attention mechanism determine how it uses that context.

In translation, each generated word depends on the encoded input and the words already produced. The decoder therefore models a sequence of conditional distributions rather than independent output words.

Setting. Let xx be an observed source sequence and y1,…,yUy_1,\ldots,y_U a target sequence, including an end-of-sequence token when termination is modeled. No independence between target tokens is assumed.

1 · Apply the conditional probability product rule

For two target tokens,

p(y1,y2∣x)=p(y1∣x)p(y2∣y1,x).p(y_1,y_2\mid x)=p(y_1\mid x)p(y_2\mid y_1,x).

Applying the same rule to a third gives a new factor p(y3∣y1,y2,x)p(y_3\mid y_1,y_2,x) . Repeating yields

pθ(y1:U∣x)=∏u=1Upθ(yu∣y<u,x).p_\theta(y_{1:U}\mid x)=\prod_{u=1}^U p_\theta(y_u\mid y_{<u},x).

This factorization is a probability identity. An encoder–decoder supplies a parameterization of its factors; a compressed context may impose an approximation to the full conditioning information.

2 · Convert a product into an additive training objective

Take negative natural logarithms:

−logpθ(y1:U∣x)=−∑u=1Ulogpθ(yu∣y<u,x).-\log p_\theta(y_{1:U}\mid x)=-\sum_{u=1}^U\log p_\theta(y_u\mid y_{<u},x).

The target history in each factor is the observed history. Supplying it during training is teacher forcing, consistent with evaluating the likelihood of the observed sequence.

3 · Expand one categorical token loss

For logits zu,kz_{u,k} over vocabulary classes kk and observed token yuy_u ,

−logpθ(yu∣y<u,x)=−zu,yu+log∑kezu,k.-\log p_\theta(y_u\mid y_{<u},x) =-z_{u,y_u}+\log\sum_k e^{z_{u,k}}.

The decoder must receive shifted targets or a mask that prevents the token at uu from directly revealing its own answer.

4 · Remove padding without changing the modeled event

For a padded batch, let mb,u=1m_{b,u}=1 for genuine target tokens and zero for padding. A mean per-token objective is

J=∑b,umb,uℓb,u∑b,umb,u.J=\frac{\sum_{b,u}m_{b,u}\ell_{b,u}}{\sum_{b,u}m_{b,u}}.

This requires at least one valid token. Averaging each sequence separately and then averaging sequences gives a different weighting when lengths differ. Both may be deliberate, but the denominator must match the intended objective.

5 · Explain generation separately

At generation time, the true prefix is unavailable. The model conditions on generated tokens and chooses or samples the next token until termination. Sampling implements the autoregressive model; greedy or beam decoding is a decision procedure over that model. Parallel teacher-forced training does not make ordinary autoregressive generation nonsequential.

Teacher forcing

During training, true previous tokens are available, so the decoder can receive yu−1*y_{u-1}^* . This is teacher forcing. At inference it cannot receive an unknown true target token, so it receives its own previous prediction instead.

The resulting mismatch is exposure bias: training histories are often cleaner than the histories encountered after an error during free-running generation. This does not make teacher forcing invalid; it identifies a difference between the conditional training calculation and the generation procedure.

Teacher forcing also does not give permission to include unavailable future target information. The autoregressive structure still determines which context each prediction is allowed to use.

The fixed-context bottleneck

An early recurrent encoder–decoder might compress the entire input into one final state. For a long source sequence, that fixed-width representation must carry every detail the decoder will later need.

Attention relieves this bottleneck by letting each decoder step retrieve from all encoder states. With additive attention,

eu,t=v⊤tanh(Wssu−1+Whht),e_{u,t}=v^\top\tanh(W_ss_{u-1}+W_hh_t),
αu,t=eeu,t∑jeeu,j,cu=∑tαu,tht.\alpha_{u,t}=\frac{e^{e_{u,t}}}{\sum_j e^{e_{u,j}}},\qquad c_u=\sum_t\alpha_{u,t}h_t.

Now the context is specific to output step uu . The decoder can emphasize different source positions at different stages instead of relying exclusively on one fixed vector.

Three architectural forms

An encoder-only model contextualizes the available input and can support classification, tagging, retrieval, or embeddings. A decoder-only model uses allowed prior context for autoregressive generation. An encoder–decoder model builds source memory and then generates while consulting it.

These are routing forms that can appear with recurrent components or Transformers. “Encoder” is not a synonym for “autoencoder”: reconstruction is a particular training objective discussed in the next chapter.

Check your understanding Why can teacher forcing create a training–inference mismatch?

During training, the decoder can receive the true previous target token. During free-running inference, it receives its own earlier prediction. An early error can therefore change later inputs in a way that was less common during training.
