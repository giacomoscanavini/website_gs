Reconstruction defines the task

An autoencoder uses an encoder and decoder with the input itself as the usual target:

z=fθ(x),x̂=gϕ(z).z=f_\theta(x),\qquad\hat x=g_\phi(z).

The encoder constructs a code zz and the decoder reconstructs the observation. This is not simply “an encoder model”; the paired reconstruction objective is essential to the definition.

For continuous data the guide uses squared reconstruction error, for example ∥x−x̂∥2/d\lVert x-\hat x\rVert^2/d . For binary or Bernoulli-modeled inputs, a binary cross-entropy reconstruction objective can be appropriate. The likelihood assumptions and feature scales matter.

A bottleneck restricts the easy identity map

If the latent dimension kk is smaller than input dimension dd , the autoencoder is undercomplete. It must prioritize information that helps reconstruction. But dimension alone does not guarantee useful semantics: a powerful nonlinear model can still memorize a finite training sample or devote its limited capacity to nuisance variation.

The source connects a linear undercomplete autoencoder with squared loss to the principal subspace of PCA. The relationship concerns a reconstruction-optimal subspace, not a guarantee that the learned latent axes have the same orientation or interpretation as a particular PCA basis.

A nonlinear decoder can represent curved structure, but additional expressiveness also changes the risks. Good training reconstruction is not enough to establish a useful general representation.

Step by step Derive the PCA connection for a linear reconstruction model

Setting. Use centered column-vector data x∈ℝdx\in\mathbb R^d with finite second moment S=𝔼[xx⊤]S=\mathbb E[xx^\top] . Consider an orthogonal rank- kk linear reconstruction x̂=UU⊤x\hat x=UU^\top x , where U∈ℝd×kU\in\mathbb R^{d\times k} and U⊤U=IU^\top U=I . This restricted problem makes the principal-subspace connection explicit.

1 · Expand the reconstruction error

Let P=UU⊤P=UU^\top . Since P⊤=PP^\top=P and P2=PP^2=P ,

∥x−Px∥2=x⊤(I−P)⊤(I−P)x=x⊤(I−P)x.\|x-Px\|^2=x^\top(I-P)^\top(I-P)x=x^\top(I-P)x.

The last equality follows from (I−P)2=I−2P+P2=I−P(I-P)^2=I-2P+P^2=I-P .

2 · Take the expectation using a trace

For a scalar, x⊤Ax=tr(Axx⊤)x^\top Ax=\operatorname{tr}(Axx^\top) . Therefore

𝔼∥x−Px∥2=tr(S)−tr(PS)=tr(S)−tr(U⊤SU).\mathbb E\|x-Px\|^2=\operatorname{tr}(S)-\operatorname{tr}(PS) =\operatorname{tr}(S)-\operatorname{tr}(U^\top SU).

The first term is constant. Minimizing error is equivalent to maximizing variance captured by the kk -dimensional subspace.

3 · Rotate into covariance eigenvectors

Write S=VΛV⊤S=V\Lambda V^\top , with eigenvalues λ1≥⋯≥λd≥0\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_d\ge0 , and put C=V⊤UC=V^\top U . Then C⊤C=IC^\top C=I and

tr(U⊤SU)=tr(C⊤ΛC)=∑i=1dλi∑j=1kCij2.\operatorname{tr}(U^\top SU)=\operatorname{tr}(C^\top\Lambda C) =\sum_{i=1}^d\lambda_i\sum_{j=1}^k C_{ij}^2.

Let ai=∑jCij2a_i=\sum_jC_{ij}^2 . Because CC⊤CC^\top is an orthogonal projector, 0≤ai≤10\le a_i\le1 and ∑iai=k\sum_i a_i=k .

4 · Allocate captured dimensions to the largest eigenvalues

For ordered eigenvalues, the largest possible weighted sum under those constraints is ∑i=1kλi\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i : if weight remains on a smaller eigenvalue while a larger one has available capacity, transferring that weight cannot reduce the sum. Choosing UU as the first kk eigenvectors attains this bound. Thus the minimum reconstruction error is ∑i=k+1dλi\sum_{i=k+1}^d\lambda_i .

5 · Avoid claiming unique latent coordinates

For unconstrained linear encoder/decoder matrices, the principal reconstruction subspace can be represented in many latent bases. Replacing an encoder by RERE and decoder by DR−1DR^{-1} for invertible RR leaves DEDE unchanged. An ordinary nonlinear autoencoder therefore neither guarantees PCA coordinates nor solves this exact orthogonal projection problem. The equivalence concerns the optimal linear squared-error reconstruction subspace under appropriate conditions.

Denoising changes the shortcut

A denoising autoencoder sees a corrupted input but reconstructs the clean target:

x̃∼q(x̃∣x),L=∥x−gϕ(fθ(x̃))∥2.\tilde x\sim q(\tilde x\mid x),\qquad L=\lVert x-g_\phi(f_\theta(\tilde x))\rVert^2.

Copying the noisy input is no longer sufficient. The model is encouraged to preserve stable structure that predicts the clean sample. This only makes sense if the corruption process represents a valid nuisance rather than changes that should alter the desired target.

For example, reconstructing a clean signal from a plausible noisy measurement has a clear interpretation. Adding arbitrary noise to strongly constrained features can instead create unrealistic training examples. The augmentation and noise chapter develops that assumption.

Step by step Derive the optimal denoising reconstruction under squared error

Setting. Let clean data be a random vector XX and corrupted input be ZZ . Train a reconstruction function gg by minimizing 𝔼∥X−g(Z)∥2\mathbb E\|X-g(Z)\|^2 . Assume the relevant conditional second moments exist. The function class is unrestricted for this population argument.

1 · Condition on a particular corrupted observation

At Z=zZ=z , a proposed reconstruction a=g(z)a=g(z) incurs conditional risk 𝔼[∥X−a∥2∣Z=z]\mathbb E[\|X-a\|^2\mid Z=z] . Define μ(z)=𝔼[X∣Z=z]\mu(z)=\mathbb E[X\mid Z=z] .

2 · Add and subtract the conditional mean

X−a=[X−μ(z)]+[μ(z)−a].X-a=[X-\mu(z)]+[\mu(z)-a].

Expanding the squared norm gives

∥X−a∥2=∥X−μ(z)∥2+∥μ(z)−a∥2+2[X−μ(z)]⊤[μ(z)−a].\|X-a\|^2=\|X-\mu(z)\|^2+\|\mu(z)-a\|^2 +2[X-\mu(z)]^\top[\mu(z)-a].

3 · Evaluate the cross term

Given Z=zZ=z , the vector μ(z)−a\mu(z)-a is fixed and 𝔼[X−μ(z)∣Z=z]=0\mathbb E[X-\mu(z)\mid Z=z]=0 . Thus the expected cross term is zero, and

𝔼[∥X−a∥2∣z]=𝔼[∥X−μ(z)∥2∣z]+∥μ(z)−a∥2.\mathbb E[\|X-a\|^2\mid z]=\mathbb E[\|X-\mu(z)\|^2\mid z]+\|\mu(z)-a\|^2.

Only the last term depends on aa , and it is minimized at a=μ(z)a=\mu(z) .

4 · Read the consequence for a denoising model

The population optimum is g*(z)=𝔼[X∣Z=z]g^*(z)=\mathbb E[X\mid Z=z] . If corruption makes several clean inputs plausible, squared-error training averages them. A blurred reconstruction can therefore follow from ambiguity and the chosen loss, not only inadequate model capacity.

5 · State the limits of the result

A finite neural network, finite training sample, or imperfect optimizer may not reach this optimum. The corruption distribution determines the conditional mean being learned. Denoising success does not alone certify anomaly detection, class separation, or a useful causal representation.

Sparse and overcomplete codes

A latent layer need not be small to be constrained. A sparse autoencoder can use many available units while penalizing frequent activation. The guide discusses a KL penalty on mean hidden activity. The constraint is on code usage rather than only on dimensionality.

This is different from dropout’s random temporary masks and from an L1 penalty on network weights. Each acts on a different object in the learning system.

Anomaly detection is conditional on reconstruction behavior

The source proposes training primarily on normal data and scoring s(x)=∥x−x̂∥2s(x)=\lVert x-\hat x\rVert^2 . A high score can flag observations that are poorly represented by the learned reconstruction system.

The key assumption is that anomalous observations reconstruct substantially worse than normal observations. An overly expressive autoencoder can reconstruct anomalies too well, and normal distribution shifts can also raise scores. A threshold therefore needs validation and a connection to the relevant false-positive or downstream cost.

A small denoising case Local worked example · Python / PyTorch

An authored gap-filling illustration using a noisy synthetic curve, a lower-dimensional code and clean reconstruction targets. A held-out validation split selects the checkpoint; test data are used only afterward. Run locally; Python is not executed by this webpage.

denoising_demo.py 
"""Authored denoising example on a synthetic one-dimensional curve in three dimensions"""

from copy import deepcopy
import torch
from torch import nn


def make_clean(n: int, generator: torch.Generator) -> torch.Tensor:
    t = 2 * torch.rand(n, 1, generator=generator) - 1
    return torch.cat([t, t.square(), torch.sin(torch.pi * t)], dim=1)


def main() -> None:
    torch.set_num_threads(1)
    torch.manual_seed(23)
    generator = torch.Generator().manual_seed(97)
    train = make_clean(768, generator)
    validation = make_clean(192, generator)
    test = make_clean(192, generator)
    noise_sd = 0.15
    validation_noisy = validation + noise_sd * torch.randn(validation.shape, generator=generator)
    test_noisy = test + noise_sd * torch.randn(test.shape, generator=generator)

    # A two-dimensional bottleneck is smaller than the three-dimensional input
    encoder = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(3, 32), nn.Tanh(), nn.Linear(32, 2))
    decoder = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(2, 32), nn.Tanh(), nn.Linear(32, 3))
    model = nn.Sequential(encoder, decoder)
    optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=0.005)
    loss_fn = nn.MSELoss()
    best_loss = float("inf")
    best_state = deepcopy(model.state_dict())
    bad_evaluations = 0
    best_step = 0

    for step in range(1, 801):
        model.train()
        noisy = train + noise_sd * torch.randn(train.shape, generator=generator)
        optimizer.zero_grad(set_to_none=True)
        loss = loss_fn(model(noisy), train)
        if not torch.isfinite(loss):
            raise FloatingPointError("Nonfinite training loss")
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()

        if step % 10 == 0:
            model.eval()
            with torch.no_grad():
                score = loss_fn(model(validation_noisy), validation).item()
            if score < best_loss - 1e-5:
                best_loss = score
                best_state = deepcopy(model.state_dict())
                best_step = step
                bad_evaluations = 0
            else:
                bad_evaluations += 1
            if bad_evaluations >= 12:
                break

    # Test data do not influence the selected checkpoint
    model.load_state_dict(best_state)
    model.eval()
    with torch.no_grad():
        baseline_mse = loss_fn(test_noisy, test).item()
        denoised_mse = loss_fn(model(test_noisy), test).item()
    assert denoised_mse < baseline_mse, "This seeded toy check should improve on the noisy input"
    print(f"Best validation checkpoint: step {best_step}")
    print(f"Test MSE of noisy input: {baseline_mse:.6f}")
    print(f"Test MSE after denoising: {denoised_mse:.6f}")
    print("Synthetic illustration only; denoising quality does not establish anomaly-detection quality")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Check your understanding Why does low reconstruction error not prove that an autoencoder learned useful semantic features?

The objective rewards reconstructing inputs, including nuisance variation. A sufficiently expressive model can memorize or copy, and even an undercomplete representation need not align with a downstream label. Reconstruction and downstream predictive usefulness are different objectives.
