Attention: queries, keys, values, and weights

Attention compares queries with keys and uses the resulting weights to combine values. Scaling, masking, and multiple heads control how that information is selected.

Attention is content-dependent retrieval

A query retrieves a weighted combination of values. Keys determine how strongly each source matches that query. The three roles are distinct even when all are projected from the same input sequence.

For X∈ℝT×dmodelX\in\mathbb R^{T\times d_{\mathrm{model}}}, form Q=XWQQ=XW_Q, K=XWKK=XW_K, and V=XWVV=XW_V. Scaled dot-product attention is

Attention(Q,K,V)=softmax(QK⊤dk)V.\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V)= \operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V.

Softmax is applied across the allowed keys for each query row. The output is a mixture of values , not a mixture of keys. The score matrix determines where to retrieve; the value matrix determines what is retrieved.

Shapes explain the operation

If QQ has TqT_q rows and K,VK,V have TkT_k rows, then QK⊤QK^\top has shape Tq×TkT_q\times T_k . Each query receives one score for each key. Multiplying the normalized scores by V∈ℝTk×dvV\in\mathbb R^{T_k\times d_v} produces Tq×dvT_q\times d_v outputs.

The number of queries need not equal the number of keys. Cross-attention can therefore connect sequences of different lengths while producing one output per query.

Step by step Derive attention gradients with respect to queries, keys, and values

Setting. For one head, let Q∈ℝT×dkQ\in\mathbb R^{T\times d_k} , K∈ℝS×dkK\in\mathbb R^{S\times d_k} , V∈ℝS×dvV\in\mathbb R^{S\times d_v} . Define A=QK⊤/dkA=QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k} , rowwise probabilities P=softmax(A)P=\operatorname{softmax}(A) , and O=PVO=PV . Assume each row has at least one allowed key. Hold any mask fixed and ignore dropout for this derivation. Let incoming gradient G=∂L/∂OG=\partial L/\partial O have shape T×dvT\times d_v .

1 · Differentiate the value mixture entry by entry

Oib=∑j=1SPijVjb.O_{ib}=\sum_{j=1}^SP_{ij}V_{jb}.

Thus the gradient with respect to one value element is ∑iGibPij\sum_iG_{ib}P_{ij} , and the gradient with respect to one probability is ∑bGibVjb\sum_bG_{ib}V_{jb} . In matrix form,

V‾=P⊤G,P‾=GV⊤.\bar V=P^\top G,\qquad \bar P=GV^\top.

Their shapes are S×dvS\times d_v and T×ST\times S .

2 · Apply the full rowwise softmax Jacobian

For fixed query row ii , ∂Pij/∂Air=Pij(𝟏j=r−Pir)\partial P_{ij}/\partial A_{ir}=P_{ij}(\mathbf1_{j=r}-P_{ir}) . Sum over all affected outputs in that row:

A‾ir=∑jP‾ijPij(𝟏j=r−Pir)=Pir[P‾ir−∑jPijP‾ij].\bar A_{ir}=\sum_j\bar P_{ij}P_{ij}(\mathbf1_{j=r}-P_{ir}) =P_{ir}\left[\bar P_{ir}-\sum_jP_{ij}\bar P_{ij}\right].

The bracket subtracts a probability-weighted row mean. This avoids constructing a dense S×SS\times S Jacobian for each row.

3 · Differentiate the scaled scores

Aij=1dk∑aQiaKja.A_{ij}=\frac1{\sqrt{d_k}}\sum_aQ_{ia}K_{ja}.

Hence

Q‾ia=1dk∑jA‾ijKja,K‾ja=1dk∑iA‾ijQia.\bar Q_{ia}=\frac1{\sqrt{d_k}}\sum_j\bar A_{ij}K_{ja},\qquad \bar K_{ja}=\frac1{\sqrt{d_k}}\sum_i\bar A_{ij}Q_{ia}.

Collecting entries gives

Q‾=A‾Kdk,K‾=A‾⊤Qdk.\bar Q=\frac{\bar AK}{\sqrt{d_k}},\qquad \bar K=\frac{\bar A^\top Q}{\sqrt{d_k}}.

4 · Continue through shared self-attention inputs

If Q=XWQQ=XW_Q , K=XWKK=XW_K , and V=XWVV=XW_V , then

X‾=Q‾WQ⊤+K‾WK⊤+V‾WV⊤,\bar X=\bar QW_Q^\top+\bar KW_K^\top+\bar VW_V^\top,
W‾Q=X⊤Q‾,W‾K=X⊤K‾,W‾V=X⊤V‾.\bar W_Q=X^\top\bar Q,\quad\bar W_K=X^\top\bar K,\quad\bar W_V=X^\top\bar V.

All three branches depend on XX , so their contributions must be added. Cross-attention has different query and key/value source tensors, so their gradient branches are accumulated to the appropriate source instead.

5 · Respect operations omitted from the derivation

Attention dropout, output projection, residual paths, and normalization contribute additional local derivatives when present. Masked positions have no score influence when their probability is fixed at zero. This derivation verifies the elementary attention operation, not every optimized kernel or complete Transformer block.

Why the square-root scale appears

For the initialization-scale argument, assume independent, zero-mean query/key components with unit variance and no cross-coordinate dependence. Summing dkd_k products gives a dot-product variance growing with dkd_k . Large logits can make softmax highly concentrated and reduce useful sensitivity. Dividing by dk\sqrt{d_k} controls this dimension-induced scale.

The assumption explains the design choice; it does not say that every trained query/key distribution is exactly independent or unit variance. Learned projections, normalization, and actual data affect the observed scores.

Step by step Derive the square-root attention scale from moments

Setting. Let s=q⊤k=∑j=1dkqjkjs=q^\top k=\sum_{j=1}^{d_k}q_jk_j . For this initialization-scale argument, assume pairs across coordinates are independent; within each pair, qjq_j and kjk_j are independent, zero mean, and have variances vqv_q and vkv_k . Real learned queries and keys need not retain these assumptions.

1 · Evaluate one product’s mean

Independence gives 𝔼[qjkj]=𝔼[qj]𝔼[kj]=0\mathbb E[q_jk_j]=\mathbb E[q_j]\mathbb E[k_j]=0 .

2 · Evaluate its second moment and variance

𝔼[(qjkj)2]=𝔼[qj2]𝔼[kj2]=vqvk.\mathbb E[(q_jk_j)^2]=\mathbb E[q_j^2]\mathbb E[k_j^2]=v_qv_k.

Since the mean is zero, this is also Var(qjkj)\operatorname{Var}(q_jk_j) .

3 · Sum the coordinate contributions

Var(s)=∑jVar(qjkj)+∑j≠rCov(qjkj,qrkr).\operatorname{Var}(s)=\sum_j\operatorname{Var}(q_jk_j) +\sum_{j\ne r}\operatorname{Cov}(q_jk_j,q_rk_r).

Coordinate independence makes the covariance terms zero. Therefore Var(s)=dkvqvk\operatorname{Var}(s)=d_kv_qv_k .

4 · Scale the standard deviation, not the variance directly

For a=s/dka=s/\sqrt{d_k} , variance scales by the square of the divisor:

Var(a)=Var(s)dk=vqvk.\operatorname{Var}(a)=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(s)}{d_k}=v_qv_k.

With unit component variances, the scaled score has variance one. Dividing by dkd_k would instead make its variance 1/dk1/d_k .

5 · Connect score scale to softmax sensitivity

For weights pi=softmax(s)ip_i=\operatorname{softmax}(s)_i , the derivative is ∂pi/∂sj=pi(𝟏i=j−pj)\partial p_i/\partial s_j=p_i(\mathbf1_{i=j}-p_j) . When one score dominates and the distribution saturates, many of these derivatives approach zero. Scaling avoids a predictable increase in score magnitude with width under the stated assumptions. It is not a proof that all trained scores have unit variance or that saturation can never occur.

A weighted-mixture example

Take q=(1,0)q=(1,0) and keys k1=(1,0)k_1=(1,0) , k2=(0,1)k_2=(0,1) , k3=(1,1)k_3=(1,1) . With dk=2d_k=2 , scaled scores are approximately (0.707,0,0.707)(0.707,0,0.707) , giving weights

α≈(0.401,0.198,0.401).\alpha\approx(0.401,0.198,0.401).

For values v1=(1,0)v_1=(1,0) , v2=(0,2)v_2=(0,2) , and v3=(1,1)v_3=(1,1) ,

α1v1+α2v2+α3v3≈(0.802,0.797).\alpha_1v_1+\alpha_2v_2+\alpha_3v_3\approx(0.802,0.797).

The second source has a lower weight but still contributes to the second output coordinate because its value has magnitude two there. A large attention coefficient alone is not a full measure of a source’s eventual effect on a prediction.

Step by step Calculate scores, normalization, and the value mixture

Setting. Use the query q=(1,0)q=(1,0), keys k1=(1,0)k_1=(1,0), k2=(0,1)k_2=(0,1), k3=(1,1)k_3=(1,1), and values v1=(1,0)v_1=(1,0), v2=(0,2)v_2=(0,2), v3=(1,1)v_3=(1,1). The key width is dk=2d_k=2.

1 · Form every dot product

q⊤k1=1(1)+0(0)=1,q⊤k2=1(0)+0(1)=0,q^\top k_1=1(1)+0(0)=1,\quad q^\top k_2=1(0)+0(1)=0,
q⊤k3=1(1)+0(1)=1.q^\top k_3=1(1)+0(1)=1.

Dividing by 2\sqrt2 gives scores (1/2,0,1/2)(1/\sqrt2,0,1/\sqrt2) .

2 · Shift the scores before exponentiating

Subtract their maximum 1/21/\sqrt2 , which leaves softmax unchanged. The shifted exponentials are

(1,e−1/2,1)≈(1,0.4930687,1).(1,e^{-1/\sqrt2},1)\approx(1,0.4930687,1).

Their sum is approximately 2.49306872.4930687 .

3 · Normalize each value

α1=α3=12+e−1/2≈0.4011121,\alpha_1=\alpha_3=\frac1{2+e^{-1/\sqrt2}}\approx0.4011121,
α2=e−1/22+e−1/2≈0.1977758.\alpha_2=\frac{e^{-1/\sqrt2}}{2+e^{-1/\sqrt2}}\approx0.1977758.

The weights are positive and sum to one by construction.

4 · Compute each output coordinate

o1=α1(1)+α2(0)+α3(1)=α1+α3≈0.8022242,o_1=\alpha_1(1)+\alpha_2(0)+\alpha_3(1)=\alpha_1+\alpha_3\approx0.8022242,
o2=α1(0)+α2(2)+α3(1)=2α2+α3≈0.7966637.o_2=\alpha_1(0)+\alpha_2(2)+\alpha_3(1)=2\alpha_2+\alpha_3\approx0.7966637.

Keys determine the weights; values supply the vectors being mixed. A key and its corresponding value do not have to be equal vectors.

5 · Handle a mask before normalization

A disallowed position receives score −∞-\infty and exponential zero. Normalize only over the remaining allowed positions. If all positions are disallowed, the mathematical denominator is zero and no probability distribution is defined by this formula; prevent that case or handle it explicitly according to the chosen implementation.

Self-attention and cross-attention

In self-attention, queries, keys, and values are derived from the same sequence. In cross-attention, queries come from one sequence and keys/values from another—typically decoder states querying encoder memory.

Masks restrict which sources are allowed. An autoregressive query must not retrieve forbidden future information. “Attention is global” should therefore be read as “direct access to the allowed interaction set,” not a requirement that every possible pair is used.

Why use multiple heads?

Each head operates in a separately projected subspace:

headh=Attention(QWQ(h),KWK(h),VWV(h)),\operatorname{head}_h=\operatorname{Attention}(QW_Q^{(h)},KW_K^{(h)},VW_V^{(h)}),
MHA(Q,K,V)=[head1;…;headH]WO.\operatorname{MHA}(Q,K,V)=[\operatorname{head}_1;\ldots;\operatorname{head}_H]W_O.

Different heads can represent different relationships, and the output projection mixes their concatenated results. An interpretable-looking attention pattern is not by itself a causal explanation of the prediction.

Check your understanding Why divide the attention dot product by the square root of the key dimension?

Under the usual independent unit-scale component argument, dot-product variance grows with the number of components. Dividing by √dₖ keeps scores on a more stable scale and reduces softmax saturation caused purely by increasing that dimension.
