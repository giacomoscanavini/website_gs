A compact gated state

The GRU combines the roles of the LSTM’s cell and hidden state into one principal recurrent state. The source uses an update gate ztz_t and a reset gate rtr_t :

zt=σ(Wzxt+Uzht−1+bz),z_t=\sigma(W_zx_t+U_zh_{t-1}+b_z),
rt=σ(Wrxt+Urht−1+br),r_t=\sigma(W_rx_t+U_rh_{t-1}+b_r),
h̃t=tanh(Whxt+Uh(rt⊙ht−1)+bh),\tilde h_t=\tanh(W_hx_t+U_h(r_t\odot h_{t-1})+b_h),
ht=(1−zt)⊙ht−1+zt⊙h̃t.h_t=(1-z_t)\odot h_{t-1}+z_t\odot\tilde h_t.

In this PDF convention , a larger update gate moves the state more strongly toward the candidate. The reset gate controls how previous-state information enters the candidate computation.

The final interpolation creates an additive route between old and new information. That is the connection to LSTM memory-path reasoning, despite the different state organization.

Work the source’s interpolation

Let ht−1=0.6h_{t-1}=0.6 , zt=0.25z_t=0.25 , and the already-computed candidate be h̃t=−0.2\tilde h_t=-0.2 . Then

ht=0.75(0.6)+0.25(−0.2)=0.40.h_t=0.75(0.6)+0.25(-0.2)=0.40.

The old state dominates because the source’s update gate is small. The example also lists a reset-gate value, but once the candidate has already been supplied, the reset value does not enter this final arithmetic again.

This separates two questions: how to construct the candidate, and how strongly to replace the state with it.

PyTorch’s documented convention

This section is an implementation supplement , not a replacement of the source equations. PyTorch writes the final interpolation as

ht=(1−zt)⊙nt+zt⊙ht−1.h_t=(1-z_t)\odot n_t+z_t\odot h_{t-1}.

Here a large ztz_t preserves the old state. The label “update gate” therefore has the opposite intuitive direction from the PDF’s displayed interpolation.

PyTorch also places the reset multiplication after the hidden affine map in the candidate:

nt=tanh(Winxt+bin+rt⊙(Whnht−1+bhn)).n_t=\tanh(W_{in}x_t+b_{in}+r_t\odot(W_{hn}h_{t-1}+b_{hn})).

The PDF instead places the reset inside the recurrent multiplication, Uh(rt⊙ht−1)U_h(r_t\odot h_{t-1}) . In general,

Uh(rt⊙ht−1)≠rt⊙(Uhht−1).U_h(r_t\odot h_{t-1})\ne r_t\odot(U_hh_{t-1}).

These are not repaired by simply renaming the gate. The official GRU reference explicitly documents this implementation distinction. It matters when comparing a hand-written cell or paper equation with framework outputs.

Step by step Work through GRU interpolation and the noncommuting reset operations

Setting. The source writes h′=(1−zs)⊙h+zs⊙nh'=(1-z_s)\odot h+z_s\odot n . PyTorch writes h′=(1−zp)⊙n+zp⊙hh'=(1-z_p)\odot n+z_p\odot h . The subscripts explicitly distinguish the gate conventions.

1 · Match the interpolation coefficients

The formulas agree for fixed h,nh,n if zp=1−zsz_p=1-z_s , because

(1−zp)n+zph=zsn+(1−zs)h.(1-z_p)n+z_ph=z_sn+(1-z_s)h.

For h=0.6,n=−0.2,zs=0.25h=0.6,n=-0.2,z_s=0.25 , this gives 0.75(0.6)+0.25(−0.2)=0.40.75(0.6)+0.25(-0.2)=0.4 . The matching PyTorch-style gate value is zp=0.75z_p=0.75 , not 0.250.25 . Equal gate numbers with swapped semantics describe different updates.

2 · Differentiate the interpolation locally

In the source convention, holding the other inputs fixed gives

∂h′∂h=diag(1−zs),∂h′∂n=diag(zs),∂h′∂zs=diag(n−h).\frac{\partial h'}{\partial h}=\operatorname{diag}(1-z_s),\quad \frac{\partial h'}{\partial n}=\operatorname{diag}(z_s),\quad \frac{\partial h'}{\partial z_s}=\operatorname{diag}(n-h).

These are partial blocks. Since nn and zsz_s themselves depend on hh , the total state Jacobian contains additional chain-rule terms.

3 · Compare the reset placement

A common textbook candidate uses U(r⊙h)=Udiag(r)hU(r\odot h)=U\operatorname{diag}(r)h . PyTorch’s candidate uses r⊙(Uh+bh)=diag(r)(Uh+bh)r\odot(Uh+b_h)=\operatorname{diag}(r)(Uh+b_h) . Even ignoring bias, Udiag(r)U\operatorname{diag}(r) and diag(r)U\operatorname{diag}(r)U are generally different matrices.

4 · Calculate a concrete counterexample

Let

U=[1234],h=[12],r=[0.20.8].U=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix},\quad h=\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\end{bmatrix},\quad r=\begin{bmatrix}0.2\\0.8\end{bmatrix}.

Reset first: r⊙h=(0.2,1.6)⊤r\odot h=(0.2,1.6)^\top , so U(r⊙h)=(3.4,7.0)⊤U(r\odot h)=(3.4,7.0)^\top . Transform first: Uh=(5,11)⊤Uh=(5,11)^\top , so r⊙(Uh)=(1.0,8.8)⊤r\odot(Uh)=(1.0,8.8)^\top . Subsequent tanh does not remove this difference.

5 · Connect the formulas to implementation comparisons

Changing gate names is enough to reconcile opposite interpolation conventions for given gates and candidates. It does not reconcile different candidate computations. The PyTorch GRU documentation explicitly describes its reset placement. Its single-layer, unidirectional bias-enabled parameter count is 3h(d+h+2)3h(d+h+2) , because it stores three input/recurrent affine blocks and two bias vectors per block.

A practical sequence-to-sequence use

The official translation tutorial uses GRUs in its encoder and decoder, with additive attention and attention visualizations. It is a complete example of the model’s role: recurrent updates build and consume sequence representations, while attention retrieves from source states.

When reading the code, follow state shapes, initialization, whether state is reset for independent sequences, and the source of decoder inputs during training versus inference. Those choices determine the actual prediction behavior more than memorizing the number of gates.

The portable understanding is the mechanism—candidate construction plus gated interpolation—together with the discipline of checking the exact convention before translating an equation into code.
