Attention is one component, not the whole model

A Transformer repeats attention and position-wise feed-forward blocks, with residual connections and normalization. The guide’s post-normalization encoder schematic is

U=Norm(X+MHA(X,X,X)),U=\operatorname{Norm}(X+\operatorname{MHA}(X,X,X)),
Y=Norm(U+FFN(U)),Y=\operatorname{Norm}(U+\operatorname{FFN}(U)),

where FFN(u)=W2ϕ(W1u+b1)+b2\operatorname{FFN}(u)=W_2\phi(W_1u+b_1)+b_2 .

Attention exchanges information between positions. The feed-forward sublayer transforms each position’s representation. Residual paths preserve a direct contribution from the block input, while normalization helps manage scale across repeated transformations.

The source notes that pre-normalization variants are common. The displayed equations remain the PDF’s post-normalization convention; practical implementations can arrange normalization differently.

6 · Compare the normalized axes, not just the names

LayerNorm typically normalizes features within each token and uses those input statistics in both training and evaluation. Training BatchNorm normalizes across batch-related axes, so a prediction and its derivatives can depend on other batch members. They are not interchangeable operations merely because both subtract a mean and divide by a scale.

Step by step Derive residual-path gradients and compare pre-norm with post-norm

Setting. For a residual transformation y=x+F(x)y=x+F(x) , let JF=∂F/∂xJ_F=\partial F/\partial x . Treat vectors as columns and assume differentiability at the point considered.

1 · Differentiate both additive branches

The identity branch has Jacobian II . Addition sums the contributions:

∂y∂x=I+JF.\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}=I+J_F.

For upstream loss gradient gyg_y , the chain rule gives

gx=(I+JF)⊤gy=gy+JF⊤gy.g_x=(I+J_F)^\top g_y=g_y+J_F^\top g_y.

The gradient therefore has a direct path that does not traverse FF .

2 · Expand two residual blocks

If x1=x0+F0(x0)x_1=x_0+F_0(x_0) and x2=x1+F1(x1)x_2=x_1+F_1(x_1) , then

∂x2∂x0=(I+JF1)(I+JF0)=I+JF0+JF1+JF1JF0.\frac{\partial x_2}{\partial x_0}=(I+J_{F_1})(I+J_{F_0}) =I+J_{F_0}+J_{F_1}+J_{F_1}J_{F_0}.

The identity term is one available path among several. The other terms can cancel or amplify it; residual connections do not prove the total gradient norm is bounded away from zero.

3 · Include normalization before the sublayer

For pre-norm y=x+F(N(x))y=x+F(N(x)) ,

∂y∂x=I+JF(N(x))JN(x).\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}=I+J_F(N(x))J_N(x).

The direct identity path remains outside normalization.

4 · Include normalization after the residual sum

For post-norm y=N(x+F(x))y=N(x+F(x)) ,

∂y∂x=JN(x+F(x))(I+JF(x)).\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}=J_N(x+F(x))(I+J_F(x)).

Now every path crosses the normalization Jacobian after the sum. These are distinct computations, even though both contain the same three ingredients.

5 · Avoid a universal architecture verdict

This path analysis explains an optimization difference, not a theorem that one layout always wins. Initial scale, depth, initialization, normalization parameters, and optimizer settings affect the complete system. A final normalization after a stack also contributes its own Jacobian.

Position must enter somewhere

Without positional signals or a fixed order-dependent mask, unrestricted self-attention is permutation-equivariant: reorder the inputs and the outputs reorder correspondingly. That symmetry is appropriate for sets but does not encode sentence order or time order by itself. A fixed causal mask is already order-dependent, so that unrestricted symmetry argument cannot be applied unchanged to every masked architecture.

The source gives sinusoidal positions:

PE(pos,2i)=sin(pos100002i/dmodel),PE(pos,2i)=\sin\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\mathrm{model}}}}\right),
PE(pos,2i+1)=cos(pos100002i/dmodel).PE(pos,2i+1)=\cos\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\mathrm{model}}}}\right).

Different dimensions vary at different frequencies. The position representation can be combined with token representations so attention can distinguish order. The guide also mentions learned, relative, and rotary approaches, without treating the sinusoidal form as a universal requirement.

Step by step Prove attention equivariance and derive a sinusoidal position shift

Setting. First consider self-attention without positional features, a fixed order-dependent mask, or stochastic dropout. Let PP be a permutation matrix, so P⊤P=IP^\top P=I and PXPX reorders token rows.

1 · Follow the permutation through the projections

For input X′=PXX'=PX , shared linear projections give Q′=PXWQ=PQQ'=PXW_Q=PQ , K′=PKK'=PK , and V′=PVV'=PV . Thus the score matrix transforms as

A′=Q′K′⊤dk=PQK⊤P⊤dk=PAP⊤.A'=\frac{Q'K'^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}=\frac{PQK^\top P^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}=PAP^\top.

2 · Follow it through rowwise normalization

Permuting a row’s entries permutes its exponentials but does not change their sum. Permuting rows simply reorders which row is normalized. Therefore

softmaxrow(PAP⊤)=Psoftmaxrow(A)P⊤.\operatorname{softmax}_{\mathrm{row}}(PAP^\top)=P\operatorname{softmax}_{\mathrm{row}}(A)P^\top.

3 · Complete the attention operation

Writing S=softmaxrow(A)S=\operatorname{softmax}_{\mathrm{row}}(A) ,

O′=PSP⊤PV=PSV=PO.O'=PSP^\top PV=PSV=PO.

The output permutes in the same way as the input: equivariance , not invariance. A shared per-token feed-forward function preserves this property; a symmetric pooling over tokens can turn it into invariance for a global prediction.

4 · Identify what breaks the premise

A fixed triangular causal mask is not generally unchanged by an arbitrary permutation, so the preceding unrestricted-attention symmetry proof does not apply to that fixed-mask computation. Position embeddings, relative-position biases, recurrence, or masking can all supply order-dependent structure. Positional encoding is a common mechanism, not the only possible one.

5 · Derive how one sinusoidal pair changes with position

For frequency ω\omega , define v(p)=(sin(ωp),cos(ωp))⊤v(p)=(\sin(\omega p),\cos(\omega p))^\top . Angle addition gives

sin[ω(p+k)]=sin(ωp)cos(ωk)+cos(ωp)sin(ωk),\sin[\omega(p+k)]=\sin(\omega p)\cos(\omega k)+\cos(\omega p)\sin(\omega k),
cos[ω(p+k)]=cos(ωp)cos(ωk)−sin(ωp)sin(ωk).\cos[\omega(p+k)]=\cos(\omega p)\cos(\omega k)-\sin(\omega p)\sin(\omega k).

Consequently

v(p+k)=[cos(ωk)sin(ωk)−sin(ωk)cos(ωk)]v(p).v(p+k)= \begin{bmatrix}\cos(\omega k)&\sin(\omega k)\\-\sin(\omega k)&\cos(\omega k)\end{bmatrix}v(p).

For a fixed offset kk , this is the same linear transformation at every pp . Using multiple frequencies gives several position scales. This algebra explains structure available to the model; it does not guarantee that a trained model extrapolates correctly to longer sequences.

Encoder and decoder visibility

An encoder normally lets input positions use bidirectional context. A causal decoder restricts each position to allowed prior context. An encoder–decoder decoder combines masked self-attention with cross-attention to the encoder’s source memory.

An additive causal mask is

Mij={0j≤i−∞j>i.M_{ij}=\begin{cases}0&j\le i\\-\infty&j>i.\end{cases}

Adding it before softmax gives zero weight to disallowed future positions. This is a mathematical additive-mask convention. Boolean mask meanings differ across APIs: scaled_dot_product_attention uses True for an allowed entry, while MultiheadAttention boolean masks use True for a blocked entry. Every query needs a valid normalization domain; an entirely masked row is not a probability distribution under the mathematical softmax formula. In the functional SDPA call, a nonzero dropout_p applies dropout even if a surrounding module is in evaluation mode, so pass zero explicitly for deterministic evaluation.

Work through the source’s dimensions

With batch size B=16B=16 , length T=100T=100 , model width 256, and eight heads, each head can have key/value width 32. The hidden tensor is 16×100×25616\times100\times256 . Per head, Q,K,VQ,K,V have shape 16×100×3216\times100\times32 , and the score matrix is 16×100×10016\times100\times100 .

Across eight heads, that is 8×16×100×100=1,280,0008\times16\times100\times100=1{,}280{,}000 attention weights in the naive explicit representation. This calculation explains where the sequence-length cost arises. Optimized kernels can change memory materialization and constants without turning the conceptual dense interaction pattern into recurrence.

Step by step Count tensor elements, matrix products, and projection parameters

Setting. Use batch B=16B=16 , length T=100T=100 , model width d=256d=256 , and H=8H=8 heads. Split widths evenly so dk=dv=d/H=32d_k=d_v=d/H=32 . Ignore padding and describe an ordinary dense attention implementation.

1 · Follow each shape

The model representation is (B,T,d)=(16,100,256)(B,T,d)=(16,100,256) . Each head uses Qh,Kh,VhQ_h,K_h,V_h with shape (16,100,32)(16,100,32) . Multiplying QhKh⊤Q_hK_h^\top gives (16,100,100)(16,100,100) scores. Across heads, the score count is

BHT2=16⋅8⋅100⋅100=1,280,000.BHT^2=16\cdot8\cdot100\cdot100=1{,}280{,}000.

At four bytes per scalar, one such tensor alone occupies 5,120,0005{,}120{,}000 bytes, before gradients, probabilities, or other activations.

2 · Count pairwise arithmetic

For one head, computing QhKh⊤Q_hK_h^\top needs BT2dkBT^2d_k multiply–accumulate terms. Summing over heads gives BT2Hdk=BT2dBT^2Hd_k=BT^2d . Multiplying attention weights by values gives another BT2dBT^2d term. This is the origin of the O(BT2d)O(BT^2d) interaction cost.

3 · Include the projections that the interaction shorthand omits

Each of the three input projections maps dd to dd for every token, costing BTd2BTd^2 terms. The output projection adds one more, for roughly 4BTd24BTd^2 projection terms. Thus full attention arithmetic includes both BTd2BTd^2 and BT2dBT^2d contributions, before the feed-forward sublayer.

4 · Count trainable parameters separately

Three dense d×dd\times d input projections plus one output projection have 4d24d^2 weights. With one bias per projected feature in each map, add 4d4d biases. For d=256d=256 , that is 262,144+1,024=263,168262{,}144+1{,}024=263{,}168 parameters. This count does not multiply by TT , BB , or HH when total projected width remains dd .

5 · Distinguish arithmetic from materialized memory

A straightforward implementation stores the quadratic score/probability arrays. Memory-efficient fused attention can avoid materializing the complete matrices while computing equivalent dense attention, so the naive storage count is not a lower bound for every implementation. The quadratic all-pairs interaction arithmetic remains distinct from this memory optimization. Actual runtime also depends on kernels, hardware, masks, and sequence lengths.

Parallel work and quadratic interactions

An RNN’s hth_t depends on ht−1h_{t-1} , creating a sequential chain. Self-attention computes all allowed query/key interactions within a layer in parallel. Dense attention’s interaction term is O(T2d)O(T^2d) , while a recurrent layer has roughly O(Tdh2)O(Td_h^2) arithmetic and sequential depth O(T)O(T) in the guide’s simplified comparison.

Long context can therefore make dense attention expensive despite its parallelism. The source motivates sparse, local, linearized, or chunked variants from this cost, rather than claiming every attention architecture has the same implementation behavior.

Training and generation are different computations

During teacher-forced causal training, all target positions are known, so a mask permits parallel loss calculation without future leakage. During generation, new tokens must be produced before they can become inputs to later predictions. Generation remains sequential at the token level.

Key/value caching avoids recomputing earlier projections each time. It reduces repeated work, not the dependency on previously generated tokens. The official SDPA and Transformer-building-blocks tutorials illustrate actual implementations; the latter is an advanced performance-oriented resource, not the required first reading.

The conceptual summary is a repeated routing-and-transformation block with explicit position and visibility rules—not merely an attention layer with more parameters.

Check your understanding Why can an autoregressive Transformer train in parallel but generate sequentially?

During training all target tokens are known, and a causal mask permits computing all position losses together without future access. During generation the next token is not known until it is produced, so later inputs depend on earlier generated outputs. Parallel training does not remove this inference dependency.
