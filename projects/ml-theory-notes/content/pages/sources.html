Reinforcement-learning supplement

The RL chapter is an external-source addition, not extracted from the four PDFs. Standard concepts are grounded in Spinning Up, the original PPO paper, and official PyTorch, TorchRL and Gymnasium material. The four derivations and deterministic return illustration are authored additions.

Complete DQN and PPO training programs remain in the linked official material. Those environment-training runs were not rerun for this update. Numerical tests check returns, Bellman consistency, terminal masks, the policy-gradient identity, baseline cancellation and PPO clipping.

Verification and expanded calculations

The original audit covers the 35 source-led chapters and their 73 mathematical walkthroughs. A separate reinforcement-learning supplement adds chapter 36 and four expandable derivations, bringing the reader to 77 calculations. Its formulas and worked values have targeted checks recorded separately in the bundle. The walkthroughs are authored expansions, not quotations from the PDFs; the four original PDF files remain unchanged.

Clarifications in the reader

The RNN gradient now writes noncommuting Jacobians in explicit order. The LSTM memory formula is identified as a direct cell-state block, not the total state derivative. Initialization distinguishes second moments from centered variance. Sampling assumptions, loss reductions, attention-mask conventions, and zero-gradient clipping are explicit.

The graph discussion separates symmetric normalization’s degree-weighted limit from row-normalized diffusion’s common limiting value. The ensemble and resampling notes distinguish model variance from irreducible observation noise, and block resampling from chronological evaluation. Appropriate assumptions remain beside each calculation rather than being implied.

How the additions were checked

Closed-form examples and derivative identities are compared with direct numerical calculations, PyTorch automatic differentiation, and finite differences where applicable. These are targeted consistency checks, not formal verification of every statement or every possible input. The bundle includes the exact test program and recorded results. Linked external training tutorials are references and were not all rerun.

Interactive support

Six original visual calculations explore activation sensitivity, quadratic-step stability, shrinkage, cell memory, attention mixtures, and graph diffusion. A seventh, in the reinforcement-learning supplement, compares immediate and delayed returns under a selectable policy. These use displayed assumptions and locally computed values, not trained-model performance. Existing PDF figures and official implementation links are retained.

How to read the source references

Each of the first 35 chapters links to the relevant sections of the supplied PDF. Page numbers mean positions in the PDF file, including its cover and contents, rather than the printed footer number. The originals are stored unchanged as separate PDF assets. Figures are crops of the original artwork, not newly generated experimental results. The reinforcement-learning chapter instead links to external primary references.

The explanatory text follows the guides’ principal mechanisms, notation and numerical examples, while grouping related sections into longer chapters. Added algebraic steps, qualifications and official-library details are identified where they go beyond the source. In particular, the different momentum, GRU, recurrent-bias and loss-normalization conventions are not silently made identical.

Official practical material

A tutorial or example script contains implemented operations or a training application. An API reference establishes the exact behavior of a component; it is not presented as an end-to-end experiment. PyTorch Geometric and Ignite are separate ecosystem packages. The classical coefficient-path, bias–variance and stacking examples come from scikit-learn rather than being forced into PyTorch.

These are selected links, not downloaded copies of the documentation. They require an internet connection. Linked tutorials may require data downloads and their own dependencies. Documentation and repositories can evolve; API-specific references in this edition point to PyTorch 2.13 documentation.

The small local companions

Four authored scripts support cases not covered by an exact official counterpart: the supplied network’s complete hand calculation, a few regularization identities, the source’s quadratic CG example, and a small synthetic denoising case. They are available under the relevant chapter rather than in a separate application catalogue.

All four ran successfully on CPU with PyTorch 2.10.0+cpu. Their numerical assertions and recorded output are included. Official third-party tutorials were reviewed as references, not rerun as part of these checks. Neither the synthetic examples nor the PDFs’ illustrative figures establish real-world predictive performance.

Offline use and publishing
